### SC-CC611-01 — Anonymous objections both change route status directly and are forbidden to do so

**Earliest owner:** D1 §4.2 F-8 and §6.1, with owner decision D-64. Downstream: core §9.9, D1 F-28 and anonymous-review annex §7.8.

**Blocking contradiction; high confidence. Independent of D-97.**

D1 F-8 first says:

> “An anonymous objection has the same semantics as any objection (F-3 to F-7)”

But its final sentence says:

> “route status changes only through attributed judgments”

The annex’s §7.8 similarly says:

> “An anonymous participant's lead changes a route's status only through an attributed judgment”

That restriction is also explicit in D-64, not merely an author’s explanatory gloss.

D1 §6.1 instead defines the route status `challenged` by:

> “some objection in `N(F({R}, [p, p]))` (F-6) is open in the route's view (Definition 2.5)”

There is no attribution-mode exception.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. An inference step S concludes K from an established premise P. Its assessment is effective and positive; its warrant is rooted and acyclic. No objection or obligation exists. S is `assessed-sound`, and K has sound support.
2. An anonymous critic records an objection O **targeting S**, in an admissible review scope. Everyone in this example may read it.
3. O is unresolved and therefore open in S’s view.
4. No judgment is submitted, replaced or otherwise changed.
5. Definition 2.5, F-6 and §6.1 now make S `challenged`; the `assessed-sound` condition no longer holds. F-28 also changes K’s challenge/support predicates.

The anonymous objection has changed route status without an attributed judgment.

This example targets S rather than K deliberately: S belongs to its own route footprint, so the route-status consequence does not depend on confusing a conclusion with a route endpoint.

**Consequence.** An implementation cannot simultaneously preserve the literal D-64/F-8 restriction and apply the mode-independent challenge predicate. Following the restriction can suppress anonymous challenges; following the predicate violates the restriction.

This is **not** a claim that an anonymous objection procedurally vetoes a decision. Core §6.9 can still accept the decision. The contradiction concerns the scientific status projection, not decision admission.

**Bounded repair.** Preserve the useful distinction:

- an anonymous objection may produce `challenged` status and the associated challenge predicates;
- it cannot itself supply an attributed validity/usability assessment;
- independently established invalidity enters through an attributed judgment;
- the core does not refuse a decision merely because the objection exists.

Have the owner clarify D-64 accordingly, then align F-8 and annex §7.8. Do not quietly reinterpret that owner decision in D3 or D4. Add a discriminator that reads route status and claim predicates immediately after the anonymous objection, before any judgment, and again after an attributed invalidity judgment.

The conflicting clauses predate 6.11. My earlier reviews did not expose this distinction.

### SC-CC611-02 — Definition 5.3 can label an unrelated relation an independent test of a claim

**Earliest owner:** D1 §5.2 Definition 5.3, constrained by Definitions 2.6 and 5.1. Downstream: evidence labels, core §9.9 and TE-05.

**Blocking definition-domain defect; high confidence in the literal missing guard. Independent of D-97.**

Definition 5.3 begins:

> “`E` is an *independent test* of `κ` within coverage `Γ` if and only if”

Its three conditions govern use history, recorded dependence provenance and coverage. None requires `κ ∈ T(E)`.

This omission matters because §5.1 explicitly fixes the targets:

> “`T` is exactly the claims the kind names”

And §6.1 explicitly requires `κ ∈ T` when defining an evidence route of κ. Definition 5.3 does not state that domain restriction.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. P and Q are unrelated predicted claims, with no ancestor-revision relationship.
2. E records `prediction-failed(P)`, with `T(E) = {P}`.
3. E’s history contains no suggesting, fitting or selecting use for Q or an ancestor of Q.
4. The provenance required by Definition 5.3 is fully recorded within Γ. The observation and the data used to develop Q have no shared leakage, instrument, code or population dependence.
5. Γ is stated and contains no unknown interval.

All three listed conditions are true for the pair `(E, Q)`. The literal “if and only if” therefore labels E an independent test of Q, although E does not target Q and has no defined bearing on Q.

This does **not** demonstrate false independent *confirmation* of Q: the additional confirming-kind requirement prevents that particular label. It demonstrates a false independent-*test* label.

**Consequence.** The formulation permits testing coverage to be overstated and makes the instruction to show the test’s bearing incomplete for this pair. Standing retrieval’s route membership restriction narrows some consumers; it does not repair the universal definition of the label.

**Bounded repair.** State the target domain explicitly: Definition 5.3 applies only to an exact target revision `κ ∈ T(E)`, under E’s recorded binding. Then apply the existing use-history, provenance and coverage conditions.

Keep neutral and unassessed independent-test cases if that remains the intended vocabulary. No new assessment requirement, score or statistical threshold is needed. Add a discriminator comparing E’s label for its actual target P with its label for unrelated Q.

The missing-provenance repair of SC68-03 remains valid. It does not close this different eligibility gap.

### SC-CC611-03 — The circularity definition has no sound empty-citation base case

**Earliest owner:** D1 §6.1, particularly “Premises” and “Well-foundedness,” with F-25. Downstream: core §9.9, TE-07 and the standing scenarios.

**Blocking formulation ambiguity/false classification; high confidence in the empty-set problem. Independent of D-97.**

The circularity rule says:

> “an inference step `S` with conclusion `κ` is **circular** when, for some premise `P` of `S`, every route that `P`'s effective supporting judgments cite reaches `κ` through inference steps.”

But the premise rule requires an effective supporting judgment; it does not require that judgment to cite an evidence relation or inference step. Definition 6.0 permits a basis of first-order source evidence, and core §5.3 imposes no nonempty route-citation requirement.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. A retained event e contains an exact, nonempty payload.
2. P says that e contains that payload. A named assessor checks the retained bytes and records an effective `supported` claim assessment Jₚ whose basis cites e directly.
3. Jₚ cites no evidence relation or inference step. It satisfies Definition 6.0: current revisions, registered ruleset, attributed assessor, no forbidden higher-order basis and no pending obligation.
4. S validly infers from P that a retained event with a nonempty payload exists. S has an effective positive inference assessment.
5. There is no path from P’s evidence back to S’s conclusion.

Let Cₚ be the routes cited by P’s effective supporting judgments. Here:

```text
Cₚ = ∅
```

The stated universal condition—every member of Cₚ reaches the conclusion—is vacuously true. Thus the literal rule marks S circular, and the route table marks it `broken`.

The source event remains an applicable root; this is not a demonstration of a rootless or genuinely cyclic argument.

**Consequence.** A source-grounded, acyclic inference can be reported as broken. Alternatively, an implementation must introduce an unstated presupposition that the cited-route set is nonempty, or an unstated requirement that every supporting claim judgment cite a route.

If direct source-grounded claim judgments are intended to be inadmissible, the present formulation must say so. The current effectiveness and premise rules admit them.

**Bounded repair.** Define the terminal-warrant cases alongside circularity:

- how a directly cited applicable event supplies a terminal basis;
- what an empty or insufficient basis means;
- when actual inference reachability establishes a cycle.

Do not treat an empty set of cited routes as evidence of a cycle. Do not solve this by letting any human assertion establish a warrant. Preserve F-25’s requirement for an applicable root and its rejection of circular support.

Add discriminators for a direct-event premise, a genuinely unsupported premise, a genuine support cycle and a cycle with an independent valid root.

This is a concrete instance of the terminal-warrant question already left open in the 6.8 ledger. It is not a defect introduced by the 6.11 objection repair.
