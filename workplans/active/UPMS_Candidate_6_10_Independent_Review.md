## 1. Serious Challenges

### SC610-01 — Disputes can never be settled for underlying claims by resolution under D-97 and Definition 2.5, creating permanent false challenge and standing deadlock

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 2.5, §6.1, F-28; downstream Core §5.11, §6.10, §9.9, and acceptance scenario T134.  
**Severity:** Blocking scientific formulation defect / permanent deadlock; high confidence.

Candidate 1.10 applies proposed decision D-97 to repair SC69-02 by defining in Definition 2.5:
> "For scientific standing, $O$ is **settled for** a record $x$ it applies through when $r(O) = \text{withdrawn}$, or when $r(O) = \text{resolved-by-assessment}$ and the resolution assessed $x$, and its resolution is not disputed (F-4); otherwise $O$ is **open for** $x$."

This rule was formulated considering ordinary claim revisions ($K_1$ carried to $K_2$), but it breaks down completely when applied to **disputes**.

Under D1 F-4 and Core §5.11 / §6.10:
- A dispute $O'$ is an ordinary objection whose target is a resolution $R$ ($\text{tgt}(O') = R$).
- Under F-4: "If an objection $O'$ targets the resolution of $O$, then $O'$ applies wherever $O$ applied..."
- Under F-6, $O'$ is included in the objection neighbourhood $N(F)$ of any footprint containing a record $O$ applied through:
  $$N(F) = \{ O : \text{bind}(O) \cap F \neq \emptyset \} \cup \{ O' : O' \text{ disputes the resolution of some } O \in N(F) \}$$
- Under F-3, a resolution decision $R_2$ that resolves dispute $O'$ cites an objection-resolution judgment whose target is $\text{tgt}(O')$, which is $R$. Thus, $R_2$ assesses $R$ (confirming the validity of $R$'s assessment of $O$).

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Claim $K$ has objection $O$.
2. Resolution decision $R$ resolves $O$, assessing target $K$.
3. Critic $C$ files dispute $O'$ targeting $R$. Under F-4, $O'$ applies wherever $O$ applied, which includes claim $K$. Under F-4, $R$ is disputed, and routes supporting $K$ are `challenged`.
4. The project convenes an independent assessment and commits resolution decision $R_2$, which resolves dispute $O'$ by assessing $R$ and confirming that $R$'s resolution of $O$ was mathematically sound. No dispute is filed against $R_2$. Critic $C$ does not withdraw $O'$.
5. Evaluate whether resolution $R$ is disputed under F-4:
   > "A resolution is disputed at a position while some objection that targets it is open for it (Definition 2.5); when every such dispute is withdrawn or resolved, it is no longer disputed..."
   For target $R$, the resolution $R_2$ assessed $R$, so $O'$ is settled for $R$. Thus, $R$ is no longer disputed.
6. Now evaluate the scientific standing of the underlying claim $K$ (D1 §6.1, F-28):
   - Under F-6, $O' \in N(F(\{K\}, [p, p])) = A(K)$. Dispute $O'$ is an objection that applies through $K$.
   - Evaluate whether $O'$ is settled for $K$ under Definition 2.5:
     - Is $r(O') = \text{withdrawn}$? **No** ($O'$ was resolved by decision $R_2$).
     - Is $r(O') = \text{resolved-by-assessment}$ and did the resolution assess $K$? **No**. Resolution $R_2$ assessed $R$ (the resolution of $O$), **not $K$**.
     - Therefore, $O'$ is **NOT settled for $K$**.
     - By the explicit fallback clause of Definition 2.5 ("otherwise $O$ is open for $x$"), **$O'$ is OPEN for $K$**.
7. Evaluate claim standing predicates (D1 F-28):
   $$\text{has-sound-support}(K) \iff \text{some route is assessed-sound and no } O \in A(K) \text{ is open for } K$$
   $$\text{has-open-challenge}(K) \iff \text{some } O \in A(K) \text{ is open for } K$$
   Because $O' \in A(K)$ and $O'$ is open for $K$:
   - `has-open-challenge(K)` is **TRUE**.
   - `has-sound-support(K)` is **FALSE**.
   - Under D1 §6.1 table, every route supporting $K$ has status **challenged**!
8. Furthermore, on every projection of $K$ or decisions citing $K$, Definition 2.5 mandates the label:
   > "resolved by assessment at $R$, not assessed for $K$"
   suggesting that resolving the dispute about $R$ somehow failed to assess $K$!

**Consequence.**  
Under D-97 as codified in Definition 2.5, **a dispute can never be settled for an underlying claim by adjudication**. Unless the filing critic voluntarily withdraws the dispute, any claim whose resolution is disputed remains permanently challenged, even after a formal resolution decision conclusively confirms the validity of the resolution. This directly contradicts Acceptance Scenario T134 ("the same holds when U is resolved") and creates an unclosable standing deadlock.

**Bounded repair.**  
In D1 Definition 2.5, define the settlement condition for disputes explicitly:
> For a dispute $O'$ whose target is a resolution $R$, $O'$ is **settled for** every record $x$ through which it applies when $r(O') = \text{withdrawn}$, or when $r(O') = \text{resolved-by-assessment}$ and the resolution assessed $R$ (settling $O'$ for $R$) and that resolution is not itself disputed. When $O'$ is settled for $R$, $O'$ ceases to be open for any record $x$ through which it applied.

### SC610-02 — D1 F-7 travel rule strictly filters out settled objections, directly contradicting Acceptance Scenario T129, Core §6.9, and the display of resolved carried objections

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-7, interacting with Core §15.1 (T129), Core §4.4, TE-04, §6.9, and §9.9.  
**Severity:** Blocking architectural / formulation contradiction; high confidence.

Candidate 1.10 repaired SC69-01 by amending D1 F-7:
> "**F-7 — Travel.** Every $O \in A(D)$ that is **open for a record of $D$'s footprint through which it applies (Definition 2.5)**, and every override covering $D$, is part of every projection of $D$, an objection resolved at another record with the label of Definition 2.5 (Gemini review SC69-01), whatever $O$'s handling, mode (open, single-blind, double-blind) or archive placement."

Core §4.4 and §15 TE-04 cite and mirror this filter:
> §4.4: "every objection in the applicable set of a decision (D1 F-6) that D1 F-7 selects appears with every projection of that decision..."  
> TE-04: "every objection in a decision's applicable set that D1 F-7 selects appears with every projection of it..."

Now evaluate the selection filter of F-7 against Core §15.1 Acceptance Scenario T129:
> "T129 | Target history (SC66-02, SC68-01, SC69-01, SC69-02): decision D1 cites K1, which has objection O; K1 is revised to K2 and O carried; decision D2 cites K2; both have O in their applicable sets, each projection naming the target through which it applies and O's target history; a resolution whose judgment assessed K2 closes O's workflow on both; **D2 shows O settled**; D1 shows O labeled 'resolved by assessment at K2, not assessed for K1', and `has-sound-support(K1)` is false..."

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Decision $D_2$ cites claim $K_2$. Its basis is $B(D_2) = \{K_2\}$.
2. The reliance footprint of $D_2$ is $F = \{K_2\}$.
3. Objection $O$ was carried from $K_1$ to $K_2$, so $\text{bind}(O) = [K_1, K_2]$.
4. Resolution decision $R$ resolves $O$, assessing $K_2$. No dispute exists.
5. Under Definition 2.5:
   $r(O) = \text{resolved-by-assessment}$ and the resolution assessed $K_2$.
   Therefore, $O$ is **settled for $K_2$**.
   By Definition 2.5, $O$ is **NOT open for $K_2$**.
6. Evaluate D1 F-7 for decision $D_2$:
   - $O \in A(D_2)$ because $\text{bind}(O) \cap F = \{K_2\} \neq \emptyset$.
   - Through what record does $O$ apply to $D_2$? It applies through $K_2$.
   - Is $O$ open for $K_2$? **No** ($O$ is settled for $K_2$).
   - Is $O$ an override? **No**.
7. Because $O$ is not open for $K_2$, **F-7 does NOT select $O$ for $D_2$'s projection**.
8. $O$ does not appear in any projection of $D_2$.

**Consequence.**  
A compliant implementation faces an irreconcilable contradiction:
- If it executes D1 F-7, Core §4.4, and TE-04 as written, decision $D_2$ completely omits $O$. Decision $D_2$ cannot show "$O$ settled", failing acceptance scenario T129.
- If it makes $D_2$ show "$O$ settled", it violates D1 F-7, which explicitly restricts selection to objections that are *open* for a record of the footprint.
- Furthermore, if an implementation widens F-7 to travel settled objections, it must specify whether *all* settled objections in history travel to decision projections, or only those whose resolution assessed a cited target.

**Bounded repair.**  
Harmonize D1 F-7, TE-04, and T129:
State explicitly in D1 F-7 that an objection travels to a decision projection if it is open for a footprint record through which it applies, **or if it was resolved by an assessment of a footprint record through which it applies**:
> **F-7 — Travel.** Every $O \in A(D)$ that is open for a record of $D$'s footprint through which it applies (Definition 2.5), or whose resolution assessed a record in $D$'s footprint through which it applies, and every override covering $D$, is part of every projection of $D$. An objection resolved at another record appears with the label of Definition 2.5; an objection settled at a footprint record appears as settled.

### Analysis of Proposed Decision D-97

Proposed decision D-97 (`UPMS_Proposed_Decisions_6_9_Review_2026-11-05.md`) proposes that:
> "A resolution settles an objection only for the target it assessed, as far as scientific standing goes. D-94 keeps one resolution record, which closes the objection's workflow everywhere... 1. **Is it sound?**  
   The foundational intent of D-97 is **sound and essential**. D-94 attempted to unify resolution into a single record while simultaneously claiming that predecessor claims would never read as settled. As proven in SC69-02, these two clauses cannot coexist without separating administrative workflow closure from epistemic standing. 2. **What fails if the owner rejects D-97?**  
   If the owner rejects D-97, the candidate texts revert to the literal text of D-94 ("one resolution settles $O$ everywhere it applies"). As a direct consequence, the counterexample trajectory of SC69-02 returns: an objectively refuted claim $K_1$ will have its `challenged` status cleared and will be reported by UPMS as having `has-sound-support(K₁) = true`, violating D1 §0.3 and the core scientific purpose of the substrate.
3. **Flaw in current draft:**  
   As revealed in SC610-01, the current drafting of D-97 failed to account for disputes of resolutions. Because a resolution of a dispute assesses the resolution $R$ and not the underlying claim $x$, the literal condition requiring assessment of $x$ leaves all resolved disputes permanently open against $x$.
4. **Recommendation to the owner:**  
   **Adopt D-97 with an explicit dispute-qualification clause** (see Bounded Repair for SC610-01).
