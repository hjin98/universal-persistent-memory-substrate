## 1. Serious Challenge

### SC-D215-01 — D2: a new plan can reset the attempt budget without preserving selected-survivor assurance

**Earliest owning domain:** D2, §10.6, “Three phases,” item 2, and “When a limit is not shown.” **Blocks ratification of the stated selected-survivor assurance.**

The within-plan repair is mathematically sound under its sampling assumptions. The remaining problem is the boundary between plans.

Section 10.6 states:

> By the union bound, the probability that any of the `A` attempts accepts a variant that fails a requirement is at most `α`, so the 95% assurance covers the variant finally selected, not only each attempt.

It then permits:

> A new plan is a new claim: it is reported with every earlier failed attempt, and it never turns a failed attempt into a pass

No operative rule binds error spending across plans pursuing the same qualification objective, or prevents an unchanged requirement and sampling population from receiving another fresh `α = 5%` allocation.

Calling the next plan a “new claim” distinguishes records. It does not, by itself, make repeated testing of the same requirement a different statistical proposition.

**Counterexample trajectory — constructed, using the candidate’s own worked-example regime:**

1. Keep the decision-basis limit at 1%, the sampling population unchanged and every attempted variant’s true confident-error rate at 1.01%.
2. Each attempt uses 603 fresh independent episodes. Other applicable requirements pass; ordinary confident errors need not be wrong-target, missing-citation or limitation-as-fact defects.
3. Plan P1 permits three attempts, each tested at `0.05/3`.
4. If all three fail, report those failures and specify P2 with three further attempts, the same limit and fresh episodes.
5. Stop at the first passing attempt. No failed attempt is relabeled, no held-out episode is reused and no limit is relaxed.

The confident-error test passes when zero or one error is observed:

```text
q = P[Binomial(603, 0.0101) ≤ 1]
  = 0.0157044498
```

Consequently:

| Search allowed | Probability of accepting at least one failing variant |
| --- | ---: |
| One plan, three attempts | 4.6377% |
| Two plans, six attempts | **9.0604%** |
| Twenty plans, sixty attempts | **61.3160%** |
| One hundred plans, three hundred attempts | **99.1337%** |

All plans can retain the same scientific requirement. The selected-survivor risk exceeds 5% without violating the stated fresh-data, fixed-size, reporting or no-retroactive-pass rules.

**Consequence:** SC-D214-03 is repaired **within one plan**, but not closed for a survivor selected across permitted plan restarts. Reporting earlier failures makes selection inspectable; it does not correct the probability.

A narrowly scoped statement—“this plan controls false acceptance over its own prespecified attempts”—remains valid. I do **not** find that the union bound fails merely because variants change between attempts.

**Bounded repair:** Define the assurance’s claim identity and restart rule in the existing §10.6 owner:

- For 95% control over the ultimately selected qualification, carry one error budget across the relevant attempt lineage, including replacement plans, using a prespecified finite allocation or another justified sequential procedure.
- Alternatively, explicitly limit assurance to each plan, state that a survivor selected across plans has **no claimed 95% selection guarantee**, and place that weaker assurance before the owner.

A changed limit must also remain a distinct, visibly post-result requirement. A pass at a relaxed limit cannot establish the former limit. Merely renaming the plan must not reset assurance for the same qualification claim.

This finding challenges the proposed D2 evaluation procedure. It does not establish that D1’s answer meanings or the ratified D2 1.3 retrieval semantics are false.

## 4. Statistical checks that survived

### 4.1 Changing variants does not invalidate the within-plan union bound

Let `H_(t−1)` contain all results available before attempt `t`. A new variant may depend on that history. If it is fixed before its fresh evaluation and the sampling/test assumptions hold, then:

```text
P(attempt t accepts a failing variant | H_(t−1)) ≤ α/A
```

The unconditional probability has the same bound. Summing over at most `A` attempts gives at most `α`. Independence between attempt outcomes is unnecessary.

For one attempt, requiring **every applicable constraint** to pass also remains sound: if the variant violates any requirement, acceptance implies falsely passing at least one requirement it actually violates. This supports the stated acceptance test, without establishing simultaneous coverage of every displayed interval.

The candidate correctly distinguishes those claims and excludes post-hoc claims for subsets that passed. SC-D215-01 concerns a separate boundary: refreshing the allocation through another plan.

### 4.2 Every listed bound has a population

The new table covers the rates tested against `ε_or`, `ε_class`, `ε_db`, `a_max`, `r_max`, `b_max`, `k_max`, `u_min`, `u_db` and `o_min`.

In particular:

- `e_wc` remains unconditional.
- `a_u` conditions on known `k*`.
- Refusal conditions on established reachability.
- Cold-start success conditions on cold-start orientation requests.
- Unknown membership cannot establish a conditional success.
- No eligible observations establish no bound.

The unknown-share ceiling does **not** prove performance on the unknown cases. Likewise, conditional `a_u` is not an unconditional abstention guarantee. Those limits must remain visible in the report.

Section 10.3’s measured latency/token percentiles and always-within-context requirement remain separate resource checks. The binomial table does not constitute a complete resource qualification design.
