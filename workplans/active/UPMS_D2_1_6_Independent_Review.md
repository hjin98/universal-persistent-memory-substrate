## 1. Serious Challenge

### SC-D216-01 — D2: separate requirement schedules do not preserve 95% assurance for the selected class qualification

**Earliest owning domain:** D2, §10.6, “One error budget per claim” and “Bounds.” **Blocks ratification of an unqualified 95% selected-qualification assurance.**

The new lineage rule correctly controls repeated false acceptance of **one requirement**. It does not generally control selection of a variant satisfying **all requirements**, because each requirement may receive its own spending schedule.

Section 10.6 defines:

> A **qualification claim** is one class with one of its requirements (§10.3).

It then provides:

> The lineage spends a total `α` = 5% by a schedule fixed before its first attempt

and concludes:

> the 95% assurance covers the variant finally selected.

The default geometric schedule is labeled an **initial default**. The operative constraint is a prespecified schedule summing to at most 5% for each requirement. No rule requires different requirements to share a schedule or otherwise bounds the combined sequence of joint acceptance opportunities.

The individual-lineage assertion is sound:

> the probability that any attempt of the lineage accepts a variant failing its requirement is at most `α`

The missing step is carrying that assurance to the selected variant’s complete class qualification.

#### Counterexample trajectory — two joint attempts for the same recall class

Use the proposed recall limits: `e_wc ≤ 5%`, `a_u ≤ 15%`, and useful-answer rate at least 60%.

Before either attempt, fix these two monotone schedules:

| Requirement lineage | Attempt 1 | Attempt 2 | Attempt 3 | Subsequent levels |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A: confident-error ceiling | 4.9% | 0.05% | 0.025% | Continue halving |
| B: unjustified-abstention ceiling | 2.5% | 1.25% | 0.625% | Continue halving |

Each schedule sums to exactly 5%. B uses the candidate’s default. A is another schedule allowed by the stated constraint. Other requirements use the default schedule.

Both attempts test **every applicable requirement**. There is no plan restart, renamed class, changed limit, post-hoc subset claim, enlarged-and-retested sample or reused episode.

1. **Attempt 1:** Evaluate 100,000 fresh independent episodes, one preselected recall request per episode. Every request has one part and known `k* = 1`. Variant V1 gives a confident wrong answer with probability `0.0500001` and otherwise answers correctly. Thus:
   - `e_wc = 5.00001%`: the variant fails A.
   - `a_u = 5.00001%`: B is satisfied.
   - Usefulness is about 95%: its floor is satisfied.

2. At A’s first level, `α_A,1 = 0.049`, the 5% confident-error bound passes exactly when the observed error count is at most **4,885**:

   ```text
   P[Binomial(100000, 0.0500001) ≤ 4885]
     = 0.0479199509863
   ```

   Whenever that condition holds, B and the usefulness bound also pass. For example, B’s upper bound at 4,885 abstentions and level 2.5% is about 5.0204%, well below 15%.

3. **If attempt 1 fails, attempt 2:** Freeze changed variant V2 and evaluate 200,000 fresh independent episodes. V2 never gives a confident wrong answer. It abstains despite `k* = 1` with probability `0.1500001` and otherwise answers correctly. Thus:
   - A is satisfied.
   - `a_u = 15.00001%`: V2 fails B.
   - Usefulness is about 85%: its floor is satisfied.

4. At B’s second level, `α_B,2 = 0.0125`, its 15% bound passes exactly when the abstention count is at most **29,642**:

   ```text
   P[Binomial(200000, 0.1500001) ≤ 29642]
     = 0.0124863207049
   ```

   On that event, the other bounds pass. In particular, zero confident errors pass A’s second level of 0.05%.

5. Select the first variant whose joint attempt passes. Both variants violate a mandatory recall requirement. With independent fresh attempts, the probability of selecting one is:

   ```text
   q1 + (1 − q1) q2
     = 0.0598079278150
     = 5.9807928%
   ```

The unknown share and budget-stop share can be zero. Grounding, support-state reporting, exact routes and other deterministic checks can hold: a grounded statement need not be correct, as D1 Definition 16.6 expressly distinguishes citation and support. This example requires neither a limitation presented as fact nor another automatic defect. The separately required scenario coverage and comparative study can remain satisfied.

I checked the critical counts through the beta-quantile bounds and independently checked both binomial probabilities by direct lower-tail summation.

#### Consequence

Each requirement’s lifetime false-acceptance probability remains below its own 5% allocation. Nevertheless, the probability that the selected class qualification accepts a variant failing **some** mandatory requirement exceeds 5%.

This is not a demand for Bonferroni correction merely because one attempt has several mandatory endpoints. For one joint attempt, acceptance implies passing any particular requirement that is false. The failure arises across attempts when **which requirement is false changes** and the requirement schedules differ.

The default schedule, applied synchronously to every requirement on every joint attempt, avoids this example. The candidate does not require that synchronization for its general assurance.

#### Bounded repair

Repair §10.6 directly. For the intended selected-class assurance, require a common spending envelope over joint qualification opportunities. A simple sufficient rule is:

- Number every joint attempt for the class across replacement plans, variants, limits, populations and operational reopening.
- Fix one schedule `γ_s` with `Σ_s γ_s ≤ 0.05`.
- Test every applicable requirement in joint attempt `s` at a level no larger than `γ_s`.

Then, conditional on the prior history, any joint attempt accepting a failing variant has probability at most `γ_s`; summing over attempts gives the intended 5% bound.

Alternatively, retain separate schedules but explicitly limit the assurance to **each requirement lineage individually**, state that this does not generally provide 95% assurance for the selected class qualification as a whole, and obtain owner adjudication of that weaker scope.

No new D1 meaning, registry or product mechanism is needed.

### 2.1 Plan replacement, changed limits and reopening

Section 10.6 now states:

> Every attempt at a claim belongs to that claim's single **lineage**, whatever plan, variant, limit, population or name it is made under.

That defeats the original SC-D215-01 trajectory. Splitting a population or workload does not create another allocation for the same class requirement. D1 Definition 16.7 fixes the five consequence classes; a workload subset is not thereby a new consequence class.

The rule also contains no reset after successful qualification or operational reopening. A subsequent qualification attempt at that requirement still belongs to its lineage.

For a fixed requirement lineage, fresh evaluation of a variant chosen using previous results can retain conditional level control. The union bound then applies without requiring independence between attempt outcomes.

Changed limits are explicitly bounded:

> a pass at it establishes only the changed limit, never the former one.

Changed populations likewise need claims scoped to the populations actually evaluated; continuing the spending lineage does not transfer performance evidence to an unevaluated population.

The broad identity is conservative rather than intrinsically unsound. Its practical cost is that materially different future populations still consume the same requirement’s allocation. The method must retain those population identities in its reports.

### 2.2 Unknown membership

Section 10.6 now states:

> A counted request whose membership in a conditional population is unknown is left out of that conditional population only

and:

> It stays in every unconditional population with whatever errors, successes and budget-limited stops are established for it

The F-D215-01 trajectory therefore fails to manufacture a pass: all 400 known confident errors among 10,000 requests remain in `e_wc`, giving 4%, regardless of their unknown `k*`.

Unknown membership supplies no conditional success evidence, and an empty eligible population establishes nothing. The unknown-share ceiling does not establish performance on unknown cases; conditional `a_u` remains a conditional claim.
