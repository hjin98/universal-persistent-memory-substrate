## 1. Serious Challenges

### SC65-01 — The shared applicability rule still loses dissent across time and repeated sharing

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-6, especially lines 187–204; downstream core §§6.8, 6.9 and 9.9.  
**Blocking; high confidence in the temporal counterexample and the missing copy-chain closure.**

The new common definition defeats SC64-01’s immediate tool-check example. Its lifecycle closure remains insufficient.

**Trajectory A: a late objection disappears without resolution.**

1. At position `p0`, decision D commits with basis `{K, S}`. S has an effective assessment J citing tool check T. No objection to T exists.
2. At `p1`, O is recorded against T. J remains effective. F-6 includes O in `N(B(D))`; D’s current projections and late-objection delta show it.
3. At `p2`, J’s assessor supersedes J with an effective assessment that does not cite T. K, S, D and O retain their exact revisions. O remains unresolved.
4. T is no longer in the current `ends(S)`. O was absent at D’s commit. Therefore neither the current neighbourhood nor the commit-time objection set retains O.

The literal calculation is:

```text
A(D,p0) = {}
A(D,p1) = {O}
A(D,p2) = {}
```

F-6 claims that an objection never leaves because the assessment through which it was reached stopped being effective. The stated rule only establishes that for objections already present at D’s commit. It does not establish it for late objections. A sibling is an objection first recorded after the historical assessment has ceased to be effective: preserving the old objection membership cannot recover an objection that did not yet exist.

**Trajectory B: sharing twice strips the original objection.**

1. K has unresolved objection O.
2. The built-in sharing operation creates C1 from K. F-6 includes O when C1 is relied on.
3. The built-in sharing operation creates C2 from C1.
4. D cites C2. Its immediate original is C1. No objection directly targets C1. The formula expands neither sharing originals recursively nor their neighbourhoods, so O targeting K is absent.

Use one scope, fully readable records and no traversal truncation. Permission coverage cannot explain the omission. This is an explicit sharing chain, requiring no semantic restatement detector.

**Consequence.** The architecture can lose a previously displayed late objection, or present a faithful second sharing copy without its original dissent. Mandatory lists faithfully enumerating the defective set do not repair either failure. These are downstream consequences of an inadequate D1 applicability definition.

**Bounded repair.** Define the relevant target footprint before selecting objections:

- Give historical decision reliance an explicit temporal meaning. Preserve the historical assessment endpoints needed to receive later objections, rather than preserving only the objections that happened to exist at commit. If later assessment intervals also create enduring applicability, derive that from immutable history, independently of which views were opened.
- Close the footprint over explicit sharing-copy links, including intermediate copies. Keep this traversal distinct from arbitrary upstream scientific inference.
- Derive `A(D)`, standing lists, disputes and the late delta from that footprint. Keep them projections; do not introduce an independently editable applicability registry.
- Add both trajectories and the “objection arrives after assessment replacement” sibling to the planned cases.

The owner must adjudicate the intended temporal meaning if replacement is supposed to terminate applicability. Such termination must be explicit and visible; it cannot coexist with the current unconditional retention explanation.

### SC65-02 — Objection coverage examines fewer targets and fewer access restrictions than applicability

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-7a, concretized by core §5.11 and §10.3.  
**Blocking; high confidence.**

F-6 now reaches route endpoints and assessment-cited tool checks. F-7a’s stated test examines scopes for records in `B(D)`, or the displayed claim `κ`. Those are different target sets.

**Trajectory A: an endpoint objection is hidden without the promised coverage statement.**

1. K and inference step S live in H. Reader P reads H and every objection home registered for H.
2. S’s effective assessment cites tool check T in Q. P authored T and can still read it through the own-record entitlement after losing Q membership.
3. Another participant’s unresolved objection O targets T and is homed in Q. P cannot read O.
4. D’s complete cited basis is `{K, S}`. Under repaired F-6, O belongs to `A(D)` through T.
5. F-7a’s stated scope test examines K and S in H. It does not require examining Q, because T is an expanded endpoint rather than a member of `B(D)`.
6. Response filtering removes O. The specified objection-coverage trigger is false.

T itself remains readable, so an unreadable-source endpoint is not a substitute warning. A broad reading of “projection’s basis” might include T, but D1 explicitly names the smaller set. That ambiguity is precisely what a common owner must eliminate.

**Trajectory B: scope access survives while record eligibility narrows.**

Record O initially readable to P in the target’s home scope. Later add a restriction tag excluding P. P still holds `read` on that scope and every registered objection home, but §7.3.4 filters O. The stated scope-access/configuration test has no corresponding condition for this record-level restriction.

The same issue needs treatment for task lineage exclusions. A configuration change retiring an objection-home registration also needs an explicit rule: forgetting a formerly used home must not remove the warning while objections homed there still apply.

**Consequence.** The strengthened claim that an unreadable applicable objection is never silent is false or materially ambiguous. The original audience-divergence family remains open in the default profile.

**Bounded repair.** Compute coverage over the same target footprint used for applicability, including historical endpoints, sharing chains and disputed-resolution targets. Define a conservative, existence-independent test for the reader’s effective ability to read objections across that footprint. Include restriction tags, lineage exclusions and retired registrations.

The test must concern where an objection *could* be hidden under configuration and eligibility rules; it must not first discover a hidden objection and then display a warning. If the first profile cannot express a sufficiently narrow safe test, use a uniform qualification for the affected profile and measure its attention cost.

D-80’s partial-coverage direction is realizable. Its present concretization does not cover its own applicability and access domains.

### R65-01 — Per-target bearing needs an explicit target-arity contract

**Owner:** D1 Definitions 2.6 and 5.1, §6.1 and F-28.  
**Medium; resolve before D1 freeze.**

**Trajectory.** An evidence relation has `T = {A, B, C}` and kind `favors(A over B | m)`. Definition 2.6 admits a target set, and §6.1 defines a route for every `κ ∈ T`. The bearing table supplies support for A and adversity for B, but no bearing for C. No explicit invariant requires `T` to equal the operands of the relation kind.

Likewise, the text should say whether several alternatives require several binary relations or a set-valued comparative kind, and how a multi-target `entails(T)` is distributed into claim-specific bearings.

**Consequence.** The intended single-target and binary cases are clear, but the domain over which the predicates are total is not. A renderer could omit C, assign a default bearing, or invent a multi-alternative interpretation.

**Bounded repair.** The simplest first contract is singleton target kinds and binary `favors` with two distinct exact claim revisions; require the target set to match the kind’s operands. Represent comparisons among several alternatives with separately identified binary relations. Alternatively define a complete per-target bearing mapping for the broader shape. Reject malformed combinations rather than assigning a default bearing.

Add a three-alternative discriminator to T119. Preserve alternative, model, purpose and carried-condition qualifiers. This needs no score or D2 method.
