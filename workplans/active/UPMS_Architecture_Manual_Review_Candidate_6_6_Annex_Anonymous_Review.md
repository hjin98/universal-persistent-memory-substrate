### 7.8 Sealed attribution and the back end of anonymous review

**Anonymity is reserved for when it is needed, and it is sealed, not unknown** (D-33 to D-36). The process (who asks, who appoints the review chair, which mode, the rounds, whether a message is a personal attack, appeals) is SCIRE's. UPMS supplies the following back-end contract. **Fixed (proposed).**

**Sealed attribution.** Every anonymous record has a true author, recorded by the core at capture like any attribution (TE-11), and stored in a sealed store keyed by review. The ordinary record carries a review-local pseudonym (for example "Critic B") stable within that review. Sealed attribution is readable only through the **review-chair role path** of that review: no participant, curator, scope manager, PI or administrator in administrator mode can read it (§7.3.6). Every sealed read is itself recorded, with the purpose and without the identity. The core uses sealed attribution internally, without disclosing it, for exactly two things:
1. **Conflict checks** (`check_conflict`): whether a proposed reviewer or review chair is the work's author, a declared collaborator, a co-author of records in the work's scope, its critic, or the PI of its project. On the critic's side, it also checks whether a proposed person chair is a declared collaborator, supervisor or co-author of any anonymous critic in the review (R-09). An open ground about the work (author, declared collaborator, co-author, PI) returns fail with its reason class. A **sealed ground** (the person is an anonymous critic in this review) and a **critic-side ground** are never reported as such: every proposed reviewer and person chair is asked to accept, and either ground makes the core decline on their behalf, which the proposer sees exactly as a voluntary decline. A sealed or critic-side decline is emitted only when the proposed person responds, or at the ordinary acceptance timeout, never sooner. The proposed person is told only "the review's conflict rules declined this appointment", the same words for both grounds (same-family review F62-11). **This refusal may depend on sealed attribution, and it is the only released fact that may** (owner decision D-79; Codex review SC64-02). A proposed person who knows their own relationships, or a proposer who knows the invitee would have accepted, can infer from it that someone with a declared relationship to that person is an anonymous critic in the review; hiding the reason or delaying the answer does not prevent this, and no further wrapper is added. **Before their first anonymous write in a review, a critic is told,** in the same words in every review, that their declared collaborators, supervisors and co-authors cannot chair it, and that if one is proposed, the refusal can let that person or the proposer infer that the critic is writing here. The critic may then write signed instead. That a declined person may be asked why they declined is a further disclosed residual.
2. **Chair appointment checks:** a person review chair must pass the conflict check, whoever assigned them; an **agent** review chair must be a fresh session of the review service with no release records from the discussion's scopes and no shared context with the discussion (§8.2). The release records make this check possible. Freshness is not neutrality: it shows that the agent saw none of the discussion, not that its operator, prompts or model family are unbiased.

Conflict and appointment checks are **core functions**, deterministic over sealed attribution, declared collaborations and recorded co-authorship. An agent review chair's model never receives a sealed identity in its inputs, so no processor sees one (SC-TE02).

**The sealing guarantee** (owner decisions D-79, D-85; TE-R13, D1 F-20a). This paragraph is the one statement of what sealed attribution may affect; the core's SE1 and every other text cite it. Each anonymous author has one pseudonym per review, so replies stay threaded to their critic and the number of distinct anonymous critics in a review is visible to its readers; the review says so before anyone writes. Every count, independence label, predicate and check result a reader can see is computed from pseudonyms and open attribution only, with one exception: the conflict-based appointment refusal above, which is disclosed to every critic before they write anonymously. For the analyst dimension of independence, two records are independent lines only if both are attributed to different principals, or both are anonymous under different pseudonyms in the same review; for any other pair, including an anonymous and a signed record, or anonymous records in different reviews, independence is "not established", whatever the sealed truth. The rule is the same for every such pair, so it reveals nothing; its cost is that an anonymous critic's agreement never counts as independent of an attributed line. An authenticated account is not proof of a unique human: counts by principal or pseudonym are bounded by the identity provider's assurance, and they say so.

**What an anonymous-role session may submit** (owner decision D-64; same-family review F62-02). Objections, critiques, replies, questions and claims that are not about a person. It never submits a judgment: `submit_sealed` refuses every record kind outside that list, and judgments in particular. An anonymous objection marks routes challenged like any objection, but an anonymous participant's lead changes a route's assessed result only through an attributed judgment, and nothing is refused because of the objection (D1 F-8, D-37, D-98).

**The anonymous role is a distinct session type** (D-49; same-family review SC-R3). A session acting in an anonymous role writes under the review's pseudonym. What it causes falls into two classes, homed differently (same-family review S62-2):
- **Published records** (objections, critiques, replies, questions and claims) are homed by the core's ordinary rule (§5.11): in the review scope, readable by every participant. They carry the pseudonym; their `owner`, `witness_principal` and source-locator fields and their provenance edges are sealed.
- **Operational records** (release records, processing records, quota and access records, working notes and checkpoints, blocked text, submission logs) are homed in the review's **sealed per-pseudonym area**.

The sealed per-pseudonym area is a record class outside scope permissions, not a scope. `set_permissions`, `create_scope`, `transfer_ownership`, administrator mode and `export` cannot reach it. It is readable only by sessions in that role for that pseudonym and through the chair path, and AC5 covers it. Otherwise a release record ("principal X's session received review package R") would unmask the critic (SC-62).

The session type has these further rules:
- **Ancestry:** what an anonymous-role session receives never becomes exposure ancestry of a write outside that role, whatever Gate 0 finds about session separation (§8.2). If the bound integration cannot show separate model contexts, critics write only through the front end's anonymous mode and the temporary review-service session.
- **Edges:** the provenance edges of sealed records are sealed. Impact traversal and conflict checks use them; every reader sees "sealed source". **Lineage exclusion treats every sealed record of a review as derived from every excluded lineage**, so a blind task never receives one, and its omission report counts them as "sealed records: n" without saying why (same-family review F62-14). The rule is the same whatever the sealed truth, so the report reveals nothing.
- **Assignment** (owner decision D-68; F62-13): while the review is open, review and deletion obligations on sealed-authored records go to the pseudonym through the chair path (§9.11, §12.3), and are never reassigned according to the sealed author's membership or employment. When the review closes, every pending and later obligation on its sealed-authored records goes to the curators of the review scope. The front end fixes this rule when it opens the review, it applies to every sealed-authored record alike, and critics are told it before they write.
- **Lifecycle acts:** withdrawal, handling changes, deletion and reads of blocked text for a sealed record are accepted only from its author's anonymous-role session while the review is open, and through the chair path after it closes.
- **Budget:** an anonymous-role session draws on a budget owned by the review and charged per pseudonym, never on the sealed principal's own limits (§8.6; R-11).
- **Logs** (owner decision D-67; F62-12): for an anonymous-role operation, every operational and transport log, including the adapter's request logs, records the pseudonym or nothing, never the principal.

**Who may assist an anonymous critic** (D-49). Only a temporary session of the review service that has no persistent scope and ends with the review round. A persistent agent principal, even the critic's own, carries memory and an overseer grant (§7.3.1, D-9) that no sealing list can cover, so the core refuses `submit_sealed` and review-package release to any session of an agent principal. Content a critic keeps outside the review, such as drafts in a personal scope, is protected only by that scope's permissions, which an administrator in administrator mode can read with a logged access record (D-1); the review tells the critic so before they write.

**The review scope.** Only the front end that opened the review holds `manage` on its review scope; no participant holds it, so no participant sees the scope's write grants, which form the set of possible critics (F62-15 c).

**Blocks and appeals.** When the front end's review chair blocks a message, the core records the block as a record attributed to the review chair (an agent chair's model and version included), withholds the message from its recipient, and keeps the blocked text sealed, readable only by its author and the review chair; it is deleted only on explicit request (D-25). An appeal from an agent chair to a human with no stake is recorded with the blocked text and without the identity. No record of blocking reveals the author. Before an appeal is sent, its author is shown that the blocked text itself may identify them. If no eligible human can hear an appeal, it stays pending and is shown as pending. A message mixing an objection to the work with a personal attack is returned whole; the author may resubmit the objection alone, and until then it is not an objection on the record. A chair found to have a conflict after appointment is replaced; sealed attribution belongs to the review, not to the chair, so a replacement loses nothing (TE-R14).

**Signing.** A critic may sign at any time through the review-chair path (`sign_sealed`). Because a pseudonym is stable within a review, signing attributes **every** published record under that pseudonym in the review: before signing, the critic is shown each of them, and signing one record alone is not offered (C61-4). From then on the published records are attributed openly; their anonymous interval stays in history (D1 F-8). The operational records in the sealed per-pseudonym area stay sealed after signing (F62-15 a). Anonymity is never lifted against the writer's will.

**What may not be anonymous.** Claims about a person (§9.13, D1 F-9), judgments (D-64) and AI critiques: the core refuses `submit_sealed` for an agent principal acting as the critic in its own right, for a claim whose `subject_kind` is person, and for any judgment.

**Masked review packages.** A blinded reviewer receives a **review package**: a derived view of the work with attribution masked and provenance withheld, while the canonical record keeps everything. It is built by the projection service and depends on its inputs like any hub. Through lineage exclusion it removes the author's **other** reasoning lineage (their rationale, notes and discussion of the work), never the work under review; masking hides identity and exclusion removes reasoning, which are different contracts (§10.6.4, D-10 extended by D-33, TE-R07). Every package states that blinding is best effort: writing style, self-citation and timing can still identify someone.

**Limits.** Sealing holds against every in-product access path. It does not hold against:
- **an operator who controls storage and keys** (§7.7). **Decided (D-40):** the first product accepts this residual and states it to every contributor before they write anonymously ("the service operator can technically read sealed identities"); revisit before any deployment whose operator the contributors do not already trust;
- **the front end that opened the review, and the transport** (owner decision D-67). A human critic authenticates to the front end, which therefore holds identity and content together; both are in the trusted computing base for sealing (§7.7), and critics are told so with D-40's disclosure.

**Until Slice 3 qualifies this contract, anonymity is unavailable:** no supposedly sealed identity is collected before then (§16).

### 11.5 Anonymous-review records

Sealing records, chair appointments with their conflict-check results, sealed reads (with purpose, without identity), blocks, returns and appeals are control records under §7.8. Participants see exactly these fields, and the full records only through the review-chair role path (same-family review R-12):

| Record | Fields participants see |
| --- | --- |
| Chair appointment | Appointer, chair (an agent chair as "review service" with its model and version), time; for a proposed person, only "accepted" or "declined" |
| Sealed read | "Chair", purpose class, day |
| Block or return | Pseudonym, chair, day, kind (personal attack, mixed message) |
| Appeal | Pseudonym, state (pending, heard, upheld, overturned) |
| Sealing record | Pseudonym and review only |
| Signing | Pseudonym, the signer's name, time |

No other field of these records is shown. The review tells participants that its write grants, and when they were used, can narrow the set of possible critics, and that only the front end that opened the review can read those grants. Their retention follows D-25.
