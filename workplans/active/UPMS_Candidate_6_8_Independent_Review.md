## 1. Serious Challenges

### SC68-01 — Per-binding unresolved status is undefined for the disputes that recursive applicability adds

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 2.5, F-4, F-6 and F-7; downstream F-28, core §§5.11, 9.9 and 10.5.1.  
**Blocking material inconsistency; high confidence.**

Definition 2.5 now says that an objection is unresolved in a projection only when it has an unresolved binding **in that projection’s footprint**. But F-6 recursively selects disputes whose bindings need not be in that footprint.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Claim K and all following records are readable in one scope. Decision D cites exactly K.
2. Objection O targets K.
3. Resolution decision R resolves O at K.
4. Unresolved objection U targets R.
5. There are no sharing copies, routes, tags, access gaps or traversal limits.

The literal sets and state are:

```text
F(B(D), [commit(D), p]) = {K}

bind(O) = {K}
bind(U) = {R}

N(F) = {O, U}

r(O, K) = resolved-by-assessment
r(U, R) is absent
```

U is an applicable objection under F-6. Nevertheless, Definition 2.5’s projection predicate is false for U: `bind(U) ∩ F` is empty. F-7 therefore does not select U as unresolved in D’s footprint.

F-4 simultaneously requires D’s projection to show the disputed resolution **with both records**. Core TE-04 and the mandatory-list guarantee require the same substantive result. The candidate supplies conflicting selection instructions.

This is not repaired by applying the new predicate to standing: F-28 and route status use the same undefined integration. Nor is it repaired by treating every dispute as permanently active. F-4 does not define how its mandatory “disputed” state clears when U is subsequently resolved or withdrawn at R.

**Consequence.** A faithful implementation must supplement or contradict the stated definition. It can omit live recursive dissent, or preserve a disputed label indefinitely after the relevant dispute has been settled. The direct per-binding lookup is coherent; its use over the recursively selected neighbourhood is not.

**Bounded repair.**

Define the binding through which each recursively reached objection applies to a projection. Preserve the connection between:

- the footprint binding that initiated propagation;
- the resolution act and bindings it resolved;
- the dispute’s own local binding, used to evaluate its disposition.

This can be derived from existing records. It does not require another persistent registry or indiscriminate expansion of the scientific reliance footprint.

Specify the clearing rule for a resolved or withdrawn dispute. Add a discriminator that checks U’s mandatory-list membership, route/claim challenge predicates and decision projection, then resolves U at R and checks the resulting state. Repeat one level deeper. T131 currently checks the coverage statement, not these status transitions.

### SC68-02 — The homing induction fails when a resolution answers another binding of the same objection

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-4/F-6’s binding-insensitive dispute closure, interacting with D-91 and D-93. The claimed proof and homing construction belong to D3 core §5.11 and proposed D-90.  
**Blocking; high confidence.**

Transitivity defeats the original H → Q → Z trajectory. It does not prove closure across all bindings of an objection.

The proof establishes:

> A resolution of O **at t** is homed among t’s possible homes.

F-6 actually follows disputes of **any resolution of O**, including a resolution at another binding. The induction silently substitutes the narrower relation for the operative one.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. H and J are separate scopes. Q is registered as an objection home for both. There are no further registrations:

   ```text
   possible_homes(H) = {H, Q}
   possible_homes(J) = {J, Q}
   ```

2. K1 lives in H. D cites K1.
3. O targets K1 and is homed in Q.
4. K1 is withdrawn. Its restatement K2 is recorded in J. O’s author rebinds O to K2.
5. The rebind passes both guards: Q is a possible home of K2, and every current reader of K2 can read Q. O now has bindings `{K1, K2}`.
6. R1 resolves O at K1 in H. R2 resolves O at K2 in J. Each resolution satisfies the new homing rule.
7. P owns K2 and R2 and initially reads J. Another participant records U disputing R2 in J.
8. J later removes P’s scope access. P still reads H and Q, and reads K2 and R2 through own-record entitlements. P cannot read U.
9. P reads D. There are no tags or lineage exclusions.

Under F-6, O enters D’s neighbourhood through K1. U then enters because it disputes a resolution of O. Under F-4 it applies wherever O applied.

But D’s footprint remains `{K1}`. P reads K1 and all its possible homes, H and Q. None of the specified coverage triggers holds. U is unreadable in J.

This trajectory obeys the current-audience rebind guard. It uses a later permitted narrowing, rather than assuming an invalid initial placement.

**Consequence.** The claim that every member of `N(F)` is homed among the footprint root’s possible homes is false. The candidate either loses an applicable dispute without the promised coverage statement or exposes a dispute-dependent status despite withholding the dispute. It also allows a dispute about the K2 resolution to characterize the independently resolved K1 binding as disputed.

Requiring a common home for all bindings **named by one resolution** does not help: R1 and R2 are separate, valid resolutions.

**Bounded repair.**

First decide whether a dispute of the K2 resolution should propagate through K1 at all. Binding-specific scientific resolution strongly suggests that it should not do so automatically.

Make F-4 and the recursive clause of F-6 follow the resolution’s relevant binding connections. Then re-prove homing and readability over that exact propagation relation, including multi-binding objections and resolutions naming several objections/bindings.

Alternatively, if all-binding dispute propagation is intentional, coverage must reach every resolution branch it admits. The present footprint-only proof cannot establish that guarantee.

Do not add another local homing exception before reconciling the propagation relation. This and SC68-01 are related manifestations of the missing binding-aware neighbourhood semantics.

### SC68-03 — Missing dependence provenance can still satisfy the independent-test and confirmation predicates

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 5.3, F-19 and Definitions 5.2/5.4. Downstream: TE-05, evidence labels and T132.  
**Blocking D1 adequacy/definition conflict; high confidence in the missing guard. Independent of D-90–D-93.**

The confirming-kind filter defeats SC67-02’s refutation example. The independence eligibility conditions remain insufficient.

Definition 5.3 tests whether shared dependence **is recorded**. F-19 says missing provenance never establishes independence. Complete chronological use history is not complete provenance about instruments, code or populations.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. A new externally reported observation has a complete declared use-history interval Γ. It has not suggested, fitted or selected K or an ancestor.
2. The observation’s instrument or calibration provenance is unknown. Its project uses are fully recorded; there is no unknown **use interval** inside Γ.
3. No shared instrument, code or population dependence is recorded, because the relevant provenance is unavailable.
4. E is recorded as `prediction-succeeded(K)`. An assessor finds it usable with limits, explicitly noting the missing instrument provenance. That assessment is effective.
5. All three literal independent-test conditions hold. The kind and effective-positive-assessment conditions for independent confirmation also hold.

Definition 5.3 permits the independent labels. F-19 forbids establishing independence from that missing provenance. The positive usability assessment does not supply the missing independence evidence.

If an unknown provenance dimension is intended to count as an “unknown interval,” that mapping must be defined. An interval of use history and an unknown dependence dimension are different things.

**Consequence.** The revised definition can turn “no dependence recorded” into an independence claim, even while another part of the same report explicitly records why independence is unknown.

T132 does not discriminate this case: it starts with complete history **and no shared dependence**, rather than unknown dependence provenance.

**Bounded repair.**

Require an attributed, coverage-bounded independence assessment, or an equally explicit completeness condition for the dependence dimensions claimed. Unknown dimensions must yield “independence not established,” while preserving the observation’s evidential value and usability.

Distinguish “eligible by reported use history” from “independence established.” An unassessed `bears-on` relation may remain a candidate evidence line; its label must not imply that independence or a discriminating test has been established.

The chosen confirming kinds are defensible as deliberately narrow product vocabulary. Keep their conditions and warrant visible; the kind filter alone does not establish independence.

### R68-01 — The identity-only late delta does not reveal a newly applicable binding of an already-known objection

**Owner:** D3 core §6.9, constrained by D1 Definition 2.5/F-6 and D-50/D-93.  
**Medium; clarify the policy-facing meaning before specification freeze.**

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. A proposed decision cites K1 and a separate restatement K2.
2. At evaluation position e, O applies through K1. The basis statement addresses O’s application to K1.
3. Between evaluation and commit, O’s author successfully rebinds it to K2. No cited basis revision changes.
4. The decision now encounters O through both bindings, including a newly unresolved application at K2.
5. The baseline and current applicable sets both contain the same objection identity O. Their set difference is empty.

The formula is mathematically determinate. It does not describe changes in binding-specific applicability or disposition.

**Consequence.** An empty late-objection delta cannot mean that no new binding-specific objection obligation arose after evaluation. Current projections may still show the changed bindings, so this is a limit of the delta’s policy-facing interpretation, not proof that O disappears entirely.

**Bounded repair.** Either derive the relevant binding-specific change information alongside the objection identities, or label the delta explicitly as **new objection identities only** and require SCIRE’s policy to compare binding/disposition changes separately. Preserve exact signatures and the no-veto boundary. Do not silently give the existing delta a stronger meaning.
