## 1. Serious Challenges

### SC66-01 — Coverage still misses the resolution-dispute branch of applicability

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-4, F-6 and F-7a; D-87’s restriction to the reliance footprint. Downstream: core §§5.11, 9.9 and 10.3.  
**Blocking; high confidence.**

The repair aligns coverage with route endpoints and sharing originals. It does not align coverage with the other expansion performed by `N(F)`: disputes of resolutions.

F-6 includes:

```text
N(F) = objections targeting F
       ∪ objections disputing resolutions of objections already in N(F)
```

F-7a checks readability and possible objection homes only for records in `F`. A resolution reached through an objection need not belong to `F`.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Scope H homes claim K and decision D, whose complete cited basis is `{K}`. Reader P reads H and every objection home registered for H. There are no lineage exclusions, restriction tags or traversal gaps.
2. After D commits, objection O is recorded in H against K.
3. P records a resolution decision R in scope Q, citing an objection-resolution judgment. Nothing requires a resolution decision to be homed in its objection’s target scope or in an objection home registered for H.
4. Q later removes P’s scope access. P continues to read R through the own-record entitlement. O and its resolution reference remain readable.
5. Another reader of Q records unresolved objection O2 against R, homed in Q. P cannot read O2.
6. F-4 and the recursive clause of `N(F)` make O2 applicable wherever O applied, including D.
7. D’s reliance footprint remains `{K}`. All three F-7a trigger conditions are false: P reads K, H and H’s registered objection homes, and has no lineage exclusion. Q is not checked.
8. Response filtering removes O2.

R itself remains readable, so an unreadable resolution source does not supply a substitute qualification.

**Consequence.** An applicable dispute can be omitted without the promised coverage statement. Rendering O as simply resolved misstates its status. Rendering “disputed” solely because unreadable O2 exists would instead disclose metadata dependent on hidden dissent; the candidate does not establish that as an exception to existence protection.

Thus the statement that an applicable objection a reader cannot read is “never silent” remains false or requires conflicting interpretations of the access rules.

**Bounded repair.**

Keep scientific reliance and objection propagation distinct. Complete the coverage calculation for every target reached by the objection/dispute expansion, including resolution decisions and further disputes.

A disclosure-safe implementation can inspect readable objection and resolution links, check possible homes at each reached target, and qualify any inaccessible branch without querying whether it contains a hidden objection. Hidden intermediate objections already require qualification at their reachable home boundary. The exact derivation belongs in the single D1 owner.

Do not add another editable applicability registry, leak a hidden dispute’s existence, or restore an objection-dependent share refusal. D-87 needs amendment or clarification: testing only `F` is insufficient while `N(F)` expands beyond it.

Add the trajectory above and a further-dispute sibling to the planned cases. T124 and T125 do not discriminate this branch.

### SC66-02 — The applicability formula does not specify how carrying and rebinding preserve historical decisions

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 2.5, F-3a, F-3b and F-6; D-47, D-63 and D-86 interact here. Downstream: core §§5.11 and 6.10.  
**Blocking material ambiguity; high confidence in the missing reconciliation.**

The enlarged historical interval preserves assessment endpoints. It does not itself preserve an objection’s former target.

F-6 selects objections by the single-valued `tgt(O)`. Core §6.10 says rebinding **moves** an objection to its successor or a restatement. Meanwhile F-3a explicitly requires a decision citing the earlier revision to keep showing O “whatever later happens to O’s target,” and F-6 says `A(D)` only grows except by explicit deletion.

**Counterexample trajectory under the current-target interpretation.**

1. O targets exact claim revision K1. D commits citing K1. All records are readable in one scope.
2. K1 is revised to K2. O is carried.
3. O’s author rebinds it to K2. The audience check succeeds; O remains unresolved.
4. D still cites K1. Its footprint contains K1, with no sharing or route expansion connecting it to K2.
5. With `tgt(O) = K2`, the literal `N(F)` calculation no longer includes O for D.

The same problem occurs when an author withdraws K1 and O is rebound to a new record restating it.

Conversely, retaining `tgt(O) = K1` does not make the formula select O for a new decision citing K2. Carrying therefore also needs a defined target-applicability relation, rather than an unexplained exception outside the purported sole definition.

**Consequence.** Two plausible implementations can disagree about the objection sets of old and successor decisions while implementing the named transitions. One silently removes an unresolved objection from an old decision. Another must supplement F-6 with an unstated historical-target or carry rule.

Interpreting `O` as every historical objection revision could preserve former targets, but the candidate does not define how those revisions collapse to one objection identity and its current handling, resolution and mode. That interpretation needs specification; it cannot be assumed to repair the formula.

**Bounded repair.**

Define target applicability from the existing committed carry/rebind history, including:

- which successor targets receive a carried objection;
- which targets cease to receive it after re-examination;
- what remains applicable to decisions that already relied on an earlier target;
- how one objection identity acquires its current state across those historical bindings.

Then make `N(F)` use that definition. Preserve F-3a’s historical-decision guarantee unless the owner explicitly changes it. No separate persistent registry is required.

Add a combined discriminator: old decision, successor decision, carry, successful rebind, and current projections of both. T108 checks carrying but does not settle F-6’s selection semantics after a successful rebind. T126 checks audience eligibility, not retention.

## 3. Other findings by earliest owning domain

### R66-01 — The late-objection delta uses a decision applicability set before that decision exists

**Owner:** D3 core §6.9, constrained by D1 F-6’s historical-read domain.  
**Medium; resolve before specification freeze.**

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. At position `e`, the front end evaluates a prospective decision with basis `{K, S}`. S’s effective assessment cites T. Existing objection O targets T; the front end considers it.
2. At later position `c`, the decision commits. Its policy result records `applicable_position = e`.
3. Nothing relevant changes between evaluation and commit.
4. At a later read `p`, §6.9 requires the difference between the decision’s applicable set at `p` and its applicable set at `e`.
5. D1 defines the latter through `[commit(D), e] = [c, e]`, although `e < c`.

That is not a historical read interval for D. If it is treated as empty, the footprint still contains basis roots but lacks their assessment endpoints, falsely making already-considered O appear late. If it is rejected as undefined, the delta cannot be computed.

**Consequence.** The repaired set-difference description does not define its baseline for the normal evaluation-before-commit path. SCIRE may receive an incorrect “not considered at evaluation” classification even when the current applicable set is correct.

**Bounded repair.** Define prospective applicability at the policy evaluation snapshot separately from the committed decision’s subsequent footprint. For example, derive the evaluation set from the exact proposed basis and its endpoints at `e`, under the pinned projection version, then subtract that derived set from current decision applicability. Preserve the chosen commit-time boundary for enduring historical reliance.

No editable set, stored delta or signature mutation is needed.

Add three controls: an endpoint objection already considered at evaluation; one arriving before commit; and an endpoint assessment effective only before commit. The last must remain outside the decision’s enduring footprint under D-86.
