## 1. Serious Challenges

### SC67-01 — D-90 does not preserve its homing argument through disputes of disputes

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-4 and F-7a, interacting with F-6; proposed D-90 and existing D-87. Downstream: core §§5.11, 6.9, 6.10, 9.9 and 10.3.  
**Blocking; high confidence.**

The direct 6.6 trajectory is refused. The recursive family remains admissible.

D-90 computes possible homes from an objection’s targets. A dispute’s target is a resolution decision. That resolution can live in a registered objection home with its **own** objection-home registrations. Those registrations need not belong to the original basis record’s possible homes.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Scope H homes claim K and decision D, whose complete cited basis is `{K}`.
2. Q is registered as an objection home for H. Z is registered as an objection home for Q. Z is **not** registered for H. At registration, the required audience inclusions hold.
3. O1 targets K and is homed in H.
4. R1 resolves O1 and is homed in Q. D-90 permits this: Q is a possible home of O1.
5. O2 disputes R1 and is homed in Q. This satisfies both the ordinary target-home rule and D-90’s special dispute rule.
6. Reader P records resolution R2 of O2 in Z, with its required judgment, basis and exposure. D-90 permits this: O2’s binding is R1, whose home is Q, and Z is registered for Q.
7. Another participant records O3 disputing R2, homed in Z. This is also permitted: Z is a possible home of O2 and is R2’s own home.
8. Z later removes P’s scope access. Permission narrowing is allowed. P still reads H and Q, and reads P’s own R2 through the own-record entitlement. All other records needed for this example can remain readable in H or Q.
9. P reads D. There are no tags, lineage exclusions, sharing copies or traversal gaps.

The result is:

```text
F(B(D), [commit(D), p]) = {K}

N(F) includes O1, O2 and O3.

Coverage checks K's possible homes: H and Q.
P reads both.

O3 is homed in Z and is unreadable to P.
The specified F-7a conditions do not trigger.
```

Registration is not transitive. Nothing in the operative rule makes `registered(Q, Z)` also register Z for H, or makes R2 inherit the original K-rooted homing boundary.

P’s own-record access to R2 prevents an unreadable resolution from supplying a substitute warning. The missing record is the further dispute.

**Consequence.** An applicable unresolved dispute can disappear without the promised coverage statement. The claim that footprint coverage structurally covers the entire dispute branch remains false. D-92’s confidentiality limitation does not itself amend the home-scope access checks or define a replacement visibility mechanism.

**Bounded repair.**

Choose one coherent owner-level construction:

- derive coverage along the readable resolution/dispute expansion, qualifying inaccessible branches from access and configuration; or
- define a root-relative homing constraint that every resolution and dispute preserves through arbitrary depth, including every binding through which it propagates.

The second option requires an actual invariant and induction over the admitted transitions. “Possible homes of the objection being resolved” does not provide it.

Multiple bindings also need reconciliation: permitting a resolution in the union of their possible homes does not prove coverage for a decision whose footprint reaches only one binding.

Do not solve this with an editable applicability registry or hidden-objection-dependent warning. Add the H → Q → Z trajectory, a cross-binding sibling and a multi-objection resolution case to the evidence specification. T128 covers only the first dispute level.

### SC67-02 — Definition 5.3 labels an independent refutation as independent confirmation

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 5.3, constrained by Definitions 5.1 and 5.4 and F-15. Downstream: core TE-05, evidence displays and use-history tests.  
**Blocking D1 adequacy defect; high confidence. Independent of D-90–D-92.**

Definition 5.3 makes “independent confirmation of κ” equivalent to three use-history and dependence conditions. It does not require that the observation confirms κ, that its relation bears positively on κ, or that an applicable warrant supports the confirmation label.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. κ is a pre-specified prediction.
2. A new observation is collected with complete provenance and stated coverage Γ. It was not used to suggest, fit or select κ or an ancestor. No relevant shared dependence exists.
3. The observation contradicts the prediction.
4. An assessor records a usable `prediction-failed(κ)` relation—or a usable `refutes(κ)` relation—with the correct warrant.
5. All three conditions of Definition 5.3 hold.

The literal definition therefore labels this relation an **independent confirmation of κ**. The repaired bearing rules simultaneously identify it as adverse.

A neutral, non-discriminating relation can satisfy the same definition. An unassessed relation is not excluded either.

**Consequence.** Faithful implementation can attach a confirming scientific label to adverse or non-confirming evidence. Correct standing predicates do not repair the separate mislabeled evidential relation. This violates the formulation’s purpose of recovering support and independence faithfully.

**Bounded repair.**

Separate the properties:

- use history and dependence establish eligibility as an independent test or evidence line within stated coverage;
- confirmation additionally requires an applicable, attributed warrant establishing confirming bearing on the exact target under its conditions.

If “independent confirmation” remains a computed label, define its confirming and usability requirements. A generic supporting-bearing test may still be too broad: adequacy for a purpose and relative preference are qualified conclusions, not interchangeable confirmation of truth.

Add controls for independent successful prediction, failed prediction, refutation, neutral consistency and absent assessment. T72 currently tests disqualifying histories without testing whether qualifying histories actually confirm anything.

### SC67-03 — D-91’s global resolution state can close an objection on a target the resolution did not answer

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 2.5 and F-3a–F-7; proposed D-91, interacting with D-47, D-63 and D-83. Downstream: core §§5.11, 6.10, 9.9 and T129.  
**Challenge to proposed semantic adequacy; high confidence in the state coupling, medium confidence in the intended product interpretation.**

Append-only bindings make the selection formula determinate. They also make every binding share one current resolution state.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. O correctly identifies a defect in K1. An earlier decision D1 cites K1.
2. K1 is revised to K2, which fixes that defect. O is carried and then rebound to K2.
3. A resolution decision R establishes precisely what F-3a permits: **the successor K2 no longer meets O**. It does not establish that the objection to K1 was mistaken or answered.
4. O’s single `resolution` field becomes `resolved_by_assessment{R}`.
5. D1’s current projection applies O through K1 but shows that global resolved state. T129 expressly requires both the old and successor decisions to show O resolved.
6. F-7 no longer requires O as unresolved dissent on K1. A later decision citing withdrawn K1 receives the same global disposition.

The binding label identifies where O entered the projection. It does not state that R answered that binding.

**Consequence.** A correct successor-specific resolution can appear as settlement of an earlier scientific objection that remains valid for the earlier record. The representation cannot directly express “answered for K2, still valid for K1.”

A related cost appears for erroneous attachment: an author who re-examines and corrects a misapplied target cannot end that target’s active applicability while keeping the objection live on the proper target. Permanent historical attachment and present scientific applicability have been identified.

A single global **administrative** disposition is coherent as a product choice. It must not silently imply scientific resolution of every attached target. The current text does not establish that distinction.

**Bounded repair.**

Preserve append-only attachment history, but bind resolution and correction acts to the targets they assess. Derive the applicable disposition for each binding from those acts. Earlier decisions retain the objection and its later disposition history without receiving an unsupported scientific closure.

An alternative first-product choice is explicitly global administrative closure, visibly labeled with the target actually assessed and with no inference that earlier targets were answered. The owner must knowingly accept that limitation and reconcile the standing and mandatory-list consequences.

Use the existing committed history; no parallel registry is needed. Extend T129 with a resolution that answers K2 only, and with a corrected misbinding. Merely asserting “both show resolved” selects the disputed semantics rather than testing its adequacy.

### R67-01 — D-92 states universal visibility that the access rules deliberately do not provide

**Owner:** D1 §8.2; core §5.11; proposed D-92.  
**Medium; reconcile before ratification.**

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. P owns claim K in H.
2. Another participant records objection O in a registered objection home Q.
3. Q later removes P’s read access.
4. P still reads K through ownership.
5. F-7a correctly supplies a configuration-based possibility warning. Access filtering withholds O’s actual existence, time and ground.

The proposed exclusion of objections requiring confidential existence is sound as a scope limitation. Its current explanation overstates what readers are guaranteed to receive.
