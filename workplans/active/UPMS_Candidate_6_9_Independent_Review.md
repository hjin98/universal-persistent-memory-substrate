### SC69-01 — The travel rule F-7 fails to select resolved cross-target objections for decision projections, contradicting Definition 2.5, Core §6.10, and T129

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-7, interacting with Definition 2.5; downstream Core TE-04, §5.11, §6.10, §9.9, and acceptance scenario T129.  
**Severity:** Blocking material contradiction; high confidence.

Candidate 1.9 Definition 2.5 establishes owner decision D-94:
> "One resolution settles $O$ everywhere it applies. A projection that shows $O$ through a target other than the one the resolution assessed says so, for example 'resolved by assessment at $K_2$', so it never reads as settling the earlier target"

Core §6.10 and acceptance scenario T129 concretize this exact guarantee:
> "decision $D_1$ cites $K_1$, which has objection $O$; $K_1$ is revised to $K_2$ and $O$ carried; $O$'s author rebinds it to $K_2$; decision $D_2$ cites $K_2$; both have $O$ in their applicable sets ... a resolution whose judgment assessed $K_2$ shows $O$ resolved on both, and $D_1$'s projection says 'resolved by assessment at $K_2$'"

However, the normative selection rule for what actually appears in a decision projection is D1 **F-7 (Travel)**:
> "Every $O \in A(D)$ with $\text{unresolved}(O)$ or a disputed resolution, and every override covering $D$, is part of every projection of $D$"

Core §5.11 and §9.9 cite and mirror this exact criterion:
> "every unresolved or disputed objection in the applicable set of a decision (D1 F-6) appears with every projection of that decision"
> TE-04: "every unresolved or disputed objection in a decision's applicable set appears with every projection of it"

Under Definition 2.5, the predicate is:
$$\text{unresolved}(O) \iff r(O) \text{ is absent}$$

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Claim $K_1$ is cited by decision $D_1$.
2. Objection $O$ targets $K_1$.
3. $K_1$ is revised to $K_2$, and $O$ is carried to $K_2$. Target history is $\text{bind}(O) = [K_1, K_2]$.
4. Resolution decision $R$ resolves $O$, assessing target $K_2$. No dispute is recorded against $R$.
5. Now, $r(O) = \text{resolved-by-assessment}(R, K_2)$ is present. Thus, $\text{unresolved}(O)$ is **false**.
6. Resolution $R$ is undisputed. Thus, $O$ does **not** have a disputed resolution.
7. A reader views decision $D_1$. Its reliance footprint is $F = \{K_1\}$.
8. Under F-6, $O \in A(D_1)$ because $\text{bind}(O) \cap F = \{K_1\} \neq \emptyset$.
9. Evaluate F-7 for $D_1$'s projection:
   - Is $\text{unresolved}(O)$ true? **No** ($\text{unresolved}(O)$ is false).
   - Does $O$ have a disputed resolution? **No**.
   - Is $O$ an override? **No**.

Under the literal text of F-7, TE-04, and Core §5.11, $O$ **is not selected for $D_1$'s projection**. $O$ vanishes completely from $D_1$. $D_1$'s projection cannot show "resolved by assessment at $K_2$" because F-7 does not travel resolved, undisputed objections to decision projections.

**Consequence.** A compliant implementation faces a direct contradiction:
- If it follows F-7, TE-04, and Core §5.11, decision $D_1$ drops $O$ entirely once $O$ is resolved at $K_2$, violating Definition 2.5, Core §6.10, and T129.
- If it includes $O$ on $D_1$, it violates the explicit filter of F-7 and TE-04, which mandate inclusion only for unresolved or disputed objections. If an implementation widens this to include all resolved objections in $A(D)$, decision projections become permanently burdened with every historically resolved objection in their one-hop footprint, defeating the purpose of F-7.

**Bounded repair.**
Amend D1 F-7 and Core §5.11 / §9.9 / TE-04 to explicitly state which resolved objections travel to decision projections:
> Every $O \in A(D)$ that is $\text{unresolved}(O)$, has a disputed resolution, or is resolved by an assessment that assessed a target other than the one through which $O$ applies to $D$, and every override covering $D$, is part of every projection of $D$.

Update T129 to verify this exact selection predicate.

### SC69-02 — D-94's global resolution settles flawed predecessor claims in route status, claim standing, and derived predicates, violating D-94's foundational warrant and D1 §0.3

**Earliest owner:** D1 Definition 2.5, §6.1, F-28; downstream Core §9.9.  
**Severity:** Blocking scientific adequacy defect; high confidence.

In owner decision D-94, the owner simplified the objection model on the explicit premise that:
> "every projection that shows it through another target says which target the resolution assessed, **so it never reads as settling the earlier target**" (D-94(c); D1 Definition 2.5)

However, D-94 simultaneously replaced per-binding resolution $r(O, b)$ with a single global predicate:
$$\text{unresolved}(O) \iff r(O) \text{ is absent}$$
accompanied by the rule:
> "One resolution settles $O$ everywhere it applies."

While D-94 intended to preserve visibility of unassessed earlier targets on decision projections via the label "resolved by assessment at $\langle\text{target}\rangle$", it failed to isolate the **scientific standing** of the unassessed predecessor claim itself.

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Claim $K_1$ states: *"Algorithm A terminates in $O(N)$ time on all graphs $G$."* Route $R_1$ supports $K_1$ with an effective positive inference assessment.
2. Objection $O$ targets $K_1$, demonstrating that cyclic graphs cause Algorithm A to loop infinitely.
3. While $O$ is unresolved, route $R_1$ has status **challenged** under D1 §6.1; predicate `has-open-challenge(K₁)` is true; and `has-sound-support(K₁)` is false.
4. The author recognizes the counterexample and publishes revision $K_2$: *"Algorithm A terminates in $O(N)$ time on all acyclic graphs (DAGs)."*
5. Objection $O$ is carried to $K_2$ ($\text{bind}(O) = [K_1, K_2]$).
6. The author commits resolution decision $R$, citing an objection-resolution judgment that assesses $K_2$ (showing cycles cannot occur in DAGs).
7. Under Definition 2.5 and D-94, $r(O)$ is now present ($\text{resolved-by-assessment}(R, K_2)$).
8. Consequently, $\text{unresolved}(O)$ becomes **false** everywhere.
9. A researcher queries the scientific standing of the original claim $K_1$:
   - **Route status (D1 §6.1):**
     $$\text{challenged} \iff \text{some objection in } N(F(\{R_1\}, [p, p])) \text{ is unresolved or has a disputed resolution}$$
     Since $\text{unresolved}(O)$ is false and $R$ is not disputed, route $R_1$ is **no longer challenged**. $R_1$ becomes **assessed-sound**.
   - **Derived predicates (D1 F-28):**
     $$\text{has-sound-support}(K_1) \iff \text{some route is assessed-sound and no } O \in A(K_1) \text{ is unresolved or disputed}$$
     $$\text{has-open-challenge}(K_1) \iff \text{some } O \in A(K_1) \text{ is unresolved or disputed}$$
     Because $\text{unresolved}(O)$ is false, `has-open-challenge(K₁)` becomes **false**, and `has-sound-support(K₁)` becomes **true**!

10. $K_1$—an objectively refuted, false scientific claim—is reported by UPMS as having sound support and no open challenges.

**Consequence.** This directly contradicts D-94's warrant ("so it never reads as settling the earlier target"). It also trips D1 §0.3's explicit falsification criterion:
> "A trajectory in which a reader, following F-1 to F-42, is shown ... a claim as having sound support when a material premise has an unresolved objection"

The administrative convenience of D-94 (a single resolution record) corrupts scientific standing: resolving an objection for a repaired revision silently validates and certifies the broken predecessor claim as sound.

**Bounded repair.**
Decouple administrative resolution closure on decisions from the scientific challenge predicate on claims and routes.
In D1 §6.1 and F-28, define an objection $O$ as challenging a route $R$ or claim $\kappa$ unless $O$ has a resolution whose assessed target **matches $\kappa$ (or a revision explicitly stated to subsume $\kappa$)**:
$$\text{settled}(O, \kappa) \iff r(O) \text{ is present and } \text{assessed\_target}(r(O)) = \kappa$$
$$\text{challenged}(R) \iff \exists O \in N(F(\{R\}, [p, p])) : \neg\text{settled}(O, \text{target}(R)) \lor \text{disputed}(r(O))$$

For claim standing, an objection whose resolution assessed a successor $K_2$ leaves $K_1$ with status `challenged` (or `challenged-by-unassessed-resolution`), preventing `has-sound-support(K₁)`. Decision projections can continue to display the labeled administrative status "resolved by assessment at $K_2$".

### SC69-03 — Dispute clearing semantics remain undefined for resolved or withdrawn disputes (unclosed core of SC68-01)

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-4, Definition 2.5, F-7, §6.1; downstream Core §6.10, §10.5.1.  
**Severity:** Blocking semantic indeterminacy; high confidence.

Candidate 1.9 change history A.19 claim C69-1 asserts that SC68-01 was fully repaired by D-94. That claim is only partially true: D-94 repaired the per-binding footprint selection defect, but **completely ignored the dispute clearing rule** identified in SC68-01:
> "F-4 does not define how its mandatory 'disputed' state clears when $U$ is subsequently resolved or withdrawn at $R$."

In Candidate 1.9 D1 F-4:
> "**F-4 — Disputed resolution.** If an objection $O'$ targets the resolution of $O$, then $O'$ applies wherever $O$ applied, and every projection that would have shown $O$ shows 'resolved by assessment, disputed' with both records."

In Core §6.10:
> "| Dispute | Any author, as an objection targeting the resolution, homed as §5.11 requires (D-94) | The original shows 'resolved by assessment, disputed' wherever it applied |"

In Core §10.5.1:
> "- it is an objection that is unresolved or whose resolution is disputed, or the target of one"

Under Definition 2.5, dispute $O'$ is an ordinary objection. It can have an author withdrawal ($r(O') = \text{withdrawn}$) or a resolution decision ($r(O') = \text{resolved-by-assessment}$).

**Counterexample trajectory.**

1. Claim $K$ has objection $O$.
2. Resolution $R$ resolves $O$.
3. Critic $C$ records dispute $U$ targeting $R$. Under F-4, $R$ shows as "resolved by assessment, disputed", and routes relying on $K$ are `challenged`.
4. Critic $C$ realizes that $U$ was based on a mathematical misreading, and withdraws $U$ under Definition 2.5 ($r(U) = \text{withdrawn}$).
5. Is resolution $R$ still "disputed"?
   - In F-4, the condition is literally: *"If an objection $O'$ targets the resolution of $O$"*. Objection $U$ still exists in history and still targets $R$. F-4 contains no clause requiring $U$ to be *unresolved*.
   - In Core §6.10, the table row states without qualification that an objection targeting the resolution causes the original to show "resolved by assessment, disputed".
   - In D1 §6.1 and F-28, route status and `has-open-challenge` check for "a disputed resolution".

**Consequence.** 
If an implementation strictly follows the text of F-4, §6.10, and §10.5.1, **a dispute can never be cleared**. Even after a mistaken dispute is withdrawn by its author or formally resolved, the target resolution remains permanently branded as "disputed", and any claim relying on it remains permanently `challenged`.
If an implementation instead clears the disputed status when $r(U)$ is present, it is inventing behavior ungrounded in the normative text, with no defined rule for how withdrawn disputes are rendered in history.

**Bounded repair.**
1. Amend D1 F-4 to define the active dispute predicate explicitly:
   > A resolution $R$ of objection $O$ is **disputed** at position $p$ if and only if there exists some objection $O'$ targeting $R$ with $\text{unresolved}(O')$ at $p$. When every dispute $O'$ targeting $R$ is resolved or withdrawn, $R$ is no longer disputed; historical views at positions prior to the settlement of $O'$ continue to show $R$ as disputed.
2. Update Core §6.10, §10.5.1, and acceptance scenario T131 to test the full lifecycle: filing a dispute, verifying the disputed state, withdrawing or resolving the dispute, and verifying that the resolution returns to settled.

### R69-01 — Ill-typed predicate in D1 F-28 (`no O ∈ A(κ) is unresolved or disputed`)

**Earliest owner:** D1 F-28; downstream Core §9.9.  
**Severity:** Medium; syntactic / semantic type mismatch.

D1 F-28 defines:
```text
has-sound-support(κ)       ⇔  some route of κ with a supporting bearing (§6.1) is assessed-sound
                               and no O ∈ A(κ) is unresolved or disputed
has-open-challenge(κ)      ⇔  some O ∈ A(κ) is unresolved or disputed
```
In D1 Definition 2.5, an objection $O$ has a handling $h(O)$ and an optional resolution $r(O)$. The predicate is $\text{unresolved}(O) \iff r(O) \text{ is absent}$. There is no predicate `disputed(O)`. A *resolution* is disputed under F-4. An objection is a dispute only if its target is a resolution. Stating that an objection $O$ is "disputed" is ill-typed.

**Bounded repair.** Align F-28 with F-7 and the route status table in §6.1:
```text
has-sound-support(κ)       ⇔  some route of κ with a supporting bearing (§6.1) is assessed-sound
                               and no O ∈ A(κ) is unresolved or has a disputed resolution
has-open-challenge(κ)      ⇔  some O ∈ A(κ) is unresolved or has a disputed resolution
```

### R69-02 — D-94 completely blocks cross-scope rebinding for all objections homed in ordinary target scopes

**Earliest owner:** D3 Core §5.11, §6.10, interacting with D-94.  
**Severity:** Low / Architectural limitation; clarify promised workflows.

Under D-94(a) and Core §5.11:
> "The possible homes of a record are its home scope and every review scope registered ... No other scope can be registered, and a review scope registers no objection homes of its own"

Core §6.10 states that a rebind is:
> "Refused unless the objection's home is the new target's home scope or an objection home registered for it"

Now consider an ordinary objection $O$ targeting claim $K_1$ in scope $H_1$. Because $H_1$ is an ordinary scope, $O$ is homed in $H_1$.
Claim $K_1$ is subsequently retracted or withdrawn, and restated in scope $H_2$ as claim $K_2$.
Can $O$'s author rebind $O$ to $K_2$ in $H_2$?
- For rebind to succeed, $O$'s home ($H_1$) must be a possible home of $K_2$.
- The possible homes of $K_2$ are $H_2$ and review scopes registered for $H_2$.
- $H_1$ is an ordinary scope, not a review scope. $H_1$ cannot be registered as an objection home for $H_2$.
- Therefore, $H_1$ can **never** be a possible home of $K_2$.
- Rebind is **always refused**.

T129 acknowledges that *"a rebind to a target of which O's home is not a possible home is refused"*. However, D1 F-3b still promises that when a target is withdrawn, an author can *"rebind it to another record (for example a new claim that restates the target)"*. Under D-94, this is impossible across scopes unless $O$ happened to be homed in a review scope registered for both scopes prior to the rebind.

**Bounded repair.** State clearly in D1 F-3b and Core §6.10 that cross-scope rebinding is supported only when the objection is homed in a shared registered review scope; for ordinary scope-homed objections, restatement in a new scope requires recording a fresh objection in that scope.

### R69-03 — Acceptance scenario T128 contains an internal contradiction regarding resolution homing and own-record entitlements

**Earliest owner:** D4 Core §15.1 (T128).  
**Severity:** Low / Specification defect.

In Candidate 6.9 §15.1, T128 was edited to enforce D-94:
> "T128 | Resolution and dispute homes (SC66-01): objection O to claim K is homed in K's scope H; a `resolution` decision for O homed in scope Q, which is not a possible home of K's objections, is refused, as is one with a restriction tag of its own; the same decision homed in H commits; a dispute O2 of it homed in Q is refused and in H is accepted; **reader P who reads H and every objection home registered for H, and reads the resolution only through an own-record entitlement, sees O2**; a reader who does not read a registered home of H sees the coverage statement, identically whether or not O2 exists (D1 F-4, F-7a; §5.11, §6.9; D-94)"

Notice the contradiction: if reader $P$ *"reads H and every objection home registered for H"*, $P$ already holds general read permissions on scope $H$. Since the resolution was committed in $H$, $P$ reads the resolution by virtue of reading scope $H$. It is contradictory to state that $P$ reads the resolution *"only through an own-record entitlement"*. This phrase was leftover residue from Candidate 6.8 (where the resolution was homed in $Q$, which $P$ did not read).

**Bounded repair.** In T128, specify that $P$ has lost read permissions on scope $H$ (or never held general read permissions on $H$) but retains an own-record entitlement to the resolution record in $H$.
