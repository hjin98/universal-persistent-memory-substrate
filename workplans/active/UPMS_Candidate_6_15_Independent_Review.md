D1 §6.1, “Premises,” now defines:

> A step's **context** is its stated conditions (`c` in Definition 2.7), the carried conditions of its conclusion, and the hypotheses it discharges: inside the step a hypothesis it discharges is an assumption in force, and beyond it the hypothesis no longer applies

Its “valid for” rule checks a supporting step’s stated and carried conditions against that context. D1 §10.3 case 44 expressly includes the discharge branch:

> `h` is in that step's context (it carries or discharges `h`)

But core §15.1 T138 still requires:

> if that step carries a hypothesis the supported step does not carry and no comparability judgment covers, the premise has no root

“Does not carry” no longer establishes absence from the consuming step’s context: the consuming step may discharge the hypothesis.

**Counterexample trajectory.** The following construction is a paraphrase of the admitted multi-step discharge case.

```text
One acyclic derivation:

U: h → B       carries h
D: B → K       discharges h; K carries no h

J_B: supported-under(h), citing U
J_K: supported, citing D

T: K → L       unconditional
```

Give U, D and T effective positive inference assessments and none negative. All other effectiveness, access and ruleset guards hold. There are no comparability judgments, objections, obligations or bypass paths.

U has no premises left to establish once its carried hypothesis is set aside.

Evaluate B as a premise of D:

1. D’s context contains h because D discharges h.
2. J_B is eligible by condition identity.
3. U is valid for D: its carried h is compatible with D’s context.
4. U supplies a valid-argument terminal.
5. B is rooted for D.
6. D exports K without h, so K is rooted for unconditional T.

This is the operative result required by D1 §6.1 and case 44. It also supplies T140’s required downstream result:

> an unconditional valid step T from K is assessed-sound

The same inner pair U/D meets T138’s contrary condition: U carries h, D does not carry h, and no comparability judgment exists. T138 therefore requires B to have no root.

**Consequence.** The same admitted records require both rooted and unrooted classifications. An implementation following T138 would reintroduce the rejected discharge failure; one following D1 and T140 would fail T138’s literal requirement.

The scope of this challenge is the acceptance contract. I found no need to reverse the new D1 context rule. Core §15.2 requires every applicable scenario to pass, so the contradictory scenario cannot remain an acceptance obligation for the exact package.
