# UPMS Owner Decisions on Retrieval, Translation and Economy, 5 November 2026 (D-101 to D-116)

5 November 2026 · decided by the product owner (Sam) · recorded by Claude (AI, Opus 5.5) · decision record, not architecture authority

**These are owner decisions on direction.** After ratifying D1 1.16 and core 6.17, the owner stopped the move to Gate 0 and asked: "Have we decided on the D2 and D3 layers yet? What algorithm and data structure are we going to implement the system? How to we ensure retrieval and writing speed of the memory as the memory grows? How do we accurately target relevant memories for each query while save compute time for memories that are not relevant?" The owner stated the requirement: "The memory can technically grow to size permitted by the storage, up to GB or even TBs. At that archival size, the system must separate memory fidelity (record completeness) and I/O efficiency and accuracy." Four rounds of discussion followed. The author's recommendations are quoted below in the author's words, shortened; the owner's answers are quoted exactly. The design note `UPMS_Retrieval_Translation_Economy_Design_Note_2026-11-05.md` sets out the approach these decisions direct.

## 2. Decisions

| ID | Decision | Recommendation the owner accepted | Owner's answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **D-101** | **Design for GB to TB now; qualify the pilot size first.** The architecture's invariants are designed for memory up to the size storage permits; qualification starts at the pilot envelope | "I recommend designing the invariants for TB scale now (planes, partitioning, segmented indexes, versioned rebuilds) because retrofitting them is costly, but qualifying only the pilot size at first." Asked: "Should the first product's architecture be designed for TB scale now?" | "Yes, and managing the exact memory record (~TB) with an approximate or lazy proxy or summary (the quick path hub), is a challenge." |
| **D-102** | **Fidelity and serving planes; a deterministic index; approximation only adds.** Canonical records are complete and never depend on an index. Retrieval rests on a deterministic tiered index (exact references, lexical, structural); approximate methods (embeddings, hubs, narratives) may add candidates and orient, never prune or establish absence; every candidate is verified exactly | The owner's own proposal ("I would propose a deterministic indexing system, where you build some sort of tiered index tree or something like a search engine"), refined by the author: "approximation may only over-include … exact with respect to a stated predicate, with coverage stating what the predicate could not see" | "Yes I accept you recommendation." and "all your proposals are a good start for us to build the system on" |
| **D-103** | **Index terms from three attributed sources:** rule-based extractors, the writing agent's declaration at write time, and versioned model extractors, normalized by the vocabulary | "I recommend all three sources, attributed, with the vocabulary normalizing them. Write-time declaration by the writing agent is the cheapest high-precision source and the one I'd lean on most." | "Yes I accept you recommendation." |
| **D-104** | **Answers are grounded in cited spans.** Every statement passed to an agent points to exact spans in canonical records; uncited hub text is labeled orientation only | "Should every answer passed to an agent be required to cite spans, with uncited hub text labelled as orientation only? I recommend yes." | "Yes I accept you recommendation." |
| **D-105** | **The index unit is the span**, rolling up to its record | "I recommend spans, so citations and verification are exact, with each span rolling up to its record." | "Yes I accept you recommendation." |
| **D-106** | **One translation layer, annotate never replace.** Ingestion (text to structure) and query compilation (request to predicates) share one schema, vocabulary and resolver; the original text is never replaced, and every structure, resolution or rendering is an attributed, versioned layer over exact spans with its basis | "The original text is never replaced. Every structure, resolution or paraphrase is an attributed, versioned layer over exact spans, with its basis, and can be corrected and re-derived." | "I accept the recommendation, and yes all your proposals are a good start for us to build the system on." |
| **D-107** | **Agents must supply context; humans may.** An agent principal's write carries a context frame and its declared referents; a human's is optional | "I recommend required for agent principals, optional for humans." | "I accept the recommendation" |
| **D-108** | **Links to people are interpretations** (D1 Definition 2.3), confirmable by the speaker and limited to identities visible in the source's scope | "I recommend they be interpretations the speaker can confirm, limited to identities visible in the source's scope." | "I accept the recommendation" |
| **D-109** | **Standalone renderings:** for agent writes, by the authoring agent at write time; for human text, in later passes by priority | "I recommend at write time by the authoring agent for agent writes, and in later passes by priority for human text." | "I accept the recommendation" |
| **D-110** | **Economy with declared loss.** Cost is spent in proportion to value; every lossy step is recorded; each answer carries a completeness status with its reason, and "cannot be confident" is a correct output | The owner's requirement ("The system that returns 'I cannot be confident because the database query is costly beyond practical' or 'I cannot be confident because reading is ambiguous or information incomplete' is acceptable and preferred. Reporting false information or overspending on confidence way beyond its importance is not acceptable."), stated by the author as "spend in proportion to value, and report what was lost in proportion to its consequence" | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-111** | **Asymmetric evaluation:** a confident wrong answer costs more than an honest abstention | "Asymmetric evaluation: confident errors are penalized more than abstentions." | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-112** | **Physical tiering is allowed** (RAM, SSD, object storage, by liveness and use); it changes latency only, never completeness or eligibility, and coverage names the tiers searched. D-25 forbids retention by time, not placement | "Physical tiering: allowed, changing latency only, never completeness." | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-113** | **Deletion at scale by key destruction (crypto-shredding)** of per-object or per-segment data keys, with lazy rewrite; subject to the KMS feasibility Gate 0 already requires | "Deletion: crypto-shredding, which ties into the KMS design." | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-114** | **Latency target:** p95 at most 1 to 2 s on hot tiers, the exact value set with the D2 cost model; cold-tier queries may be slower and are labeled | "Per-query latency targets: p95 ≤ 1–2 s on hot tiers." | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-115** | **Escalation:** an agent may escalate a search within its task budget on its own; beyond it, it asks a human, with a cost estimate | "I recommend that an agent may escalate within its task budget on its own, and must ask a human with a cost estimate before going beyond it." | "Yes, I accept your recommendations for all." |
| **D-116** | **Procedural discovery is the operating rule of thumb:** when the target is unknown, retrieve broadly and cheaply, read the overview, then narrow into a specific domain in depth, until the target is hit or the investigation is satisfied | The owner's own principle: "First, a broad retrieval. Then, the investigator sees the overview and what's important, the weight estimate (confidence or probability estimates) changes, and the investigator now narrow down and query a specific domain in depth, until the search hits the target or satisfies the investigation. This is true more generally for human and AI agents, so this 'procedural discovery' should be an optimization rule-of-thumb for operation, I think." | Stated by the owner |

The author's completions of D-116 (the broad step returns exact counts as well as the hub; unexplored branches are reported, never treated as excluded; working estimates are never stored as confidence; the trail is kept for resumption) are proposals in the design note §8, which the owner may overrule.

## 3. Consequences for authority

- **SERIOUS CHALLENGE to accepted D3 (adequacy for D-101).** Core 6.17 sizes the pilot at 10 GiB of payloads and 1 million records, says "beyond this, backpressure, archive review and requalification" (§14.2), and leaves "Partition and filter strategy" to D4 (§10.1). It has no scaling design and no fidelity–serving invariant, so it does not meet D-101. This is routed to D3 (a core revision), with a D1 addendum and a new D2; it is not patched in D4.
- **D2 opens.** Core §1.4 and D1 §9 say no D2 parent is required. None of their listed triggers fires under these decisions (no stored score, no guaranteed approximation bound), but D-101, D-110, D-111 and D-114 put requirements on estimators, error models and cost models (extraction accuracy, recall at scale, cost before execution), which SSDP assigns to D2. The domain determination changes by this owner direction.
- **Slice content moves.** Deterministic extractors, write-time index terms, context frames, the exact-reference and lexical tiers, completeness statuses and grounding belong in Slice 1a-core, ahead of the checking annex's Slice 3.
- **Gate 0 sequence.** The D1 addendum, the D2 candidate and the core revision need independent review outside the Claude family and the owner's ratification before the Slice 1a-core D4 specification is written.
