## 2. D2: closure of SC-D216-01 and the single lineage

### 2.1 The operative repair supplies the missing common envelope

Section 10.6, phase 2, now states:

> All qualification attempts for the product form one **lineage**, numbered `s` = 1, 2, … across every plan, variant, limit, population, class set, name and reopening in operation (§14).

It requires:

> every bound of every declared requirement is taken at the attempt's single level `α_s`, from one schedule fixed before the first attempt with `Σ α_s ≤ α`, `α` = 5%

and:

> The attempt accepts its declared class set only if every one of those bounds passes, and otherwise accepts nothing.

These are operative constraints, not merely change-history assurances. Separate requirement schedules, separate class allocations and successful-subset salvage are no longer permitted within one attempt.

Section 10.3 still requires:

> every requirement of that class holds as decided in §10.6

Thus declaring a class does not authorize dropping an inconvenient mandatory requirement of that class.

### 2.2 Why the selected-qualification bound now follows

Let `H_(s−1)` contain everything known before attempt `s`, including earlier outcomes and the frozen variant, limits, sampling design and declared scope selected for this attempt.

Let `E_s` mean that attempt `s` accepts although at least one of its declared, statistically tested rate requirements is false.

For any history under which a declared requirement is false, choose one such requirement before seeing this attempt’s results. Joint acceptance implies that requirement’s bound passes. Under the fixed experiment’s binomial sampling assumptions:

```text
P(E_s | H_(s−1)) ≤ α_s.
```

Therefore:

```text
P(any E_s) ≤ Σ_s P(E_s) ≤ Σ_s α_s ≤ 0.05.
```

Changing which requirement is false between attempts does not break this argument: all requirements in attempt `s` use the same envelope `α_s`. Changing the class set likewise changes the tested conjunction, without creating another allocation.

Independence between requirements or between attempt outcomes is unnecessary for this union-bound argument. Valid conditional sampling and testing within each fresh attempt remain necessary.

The assurance concerns false acceptance of the declared rate requirements. As §10.6 expressly distinguishes, deterministic requirements remain case-by-case checks. This is neither simultaneous coverage of all reported intervals nor a probability that every future response satisfies every structural invariant.

### 2.3 The original counterexample no longer exceeds 5%

I retained the two constructed variants, populations and sample sizes from SC-D216-01:

- V1: 100,000 recall requests; true confident-error rate `0.0500001`.
- V2: 200,000 recall requests; true unjustified-abstention rate `0.1500001`.
- Other requirements hold as in the original construction.

The former example combined a 4.9% first-attempt level for one requirement with a 1.25% second-attempt level for another, under separate schedules. That combination is inadmissible now.

Recomputing the trajectory under two admissible common schedules gives:

| Common schedule | Attempt 1 passing probability | Attempt 2 passing probability | Probability of selecting a failing variant |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Default: 2.5%, 1.25%, … | 2.4275826% | 1.2486321% | **3.6459031%** |
| 4.9%, 0.05%, 0.025%, … | 4.7919951% | 0.0497597% | **4.8393703%** |

The critical counts are respectively `(4864, 29642)` and `(4885, 29475)`. I checked the binomial probabilities independently by direct lower-tail summation.

**Consequence:** The counterexample’s selection mechanism remains possible, but its false-acceptance probability is controlled by the common allocation. No further repair is needed for SC-D216-01.
