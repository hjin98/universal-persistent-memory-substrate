## 2. Serious Challenges

### SC620-01 — D2: zero-sample admission still lacks an elapsed-time prediction

**Earliest owning domain:** D2. **Residual of SC619-01; blocking for D2 and its D3 consumer.**

D2 §7.1 now gives work a coherent unit:

> Engine work `W` is measured in **CPU-seconds** on the bound deployment, and every coefficient `c_*` in CPU-seconds per operation.

It separately defines elapsed-time prediction:

> it is **predicted** as `t̂(π) = g_f(W(π))`, where `g_f` is a predictor fitted per plan family `f` to measured `(W, t)` pairs on the development workload (initially a linear fit)

D2 §7.2 item 1 then says:

> **Cold start:** until a family has at least 100 measured runs (**initial default**), its interval is stated as "unknown" and admission uses the point prediction from the coefficients alone.

This supplies an interval-status rule but does not initialize `g_f` when the family has no measured pairs. The coefficients produce CPU-seconds. They do not, by themselves, select a prediction of elapsed seconds. No bootstrap mapping, borrowed calibrated predictor, initialization parameters or unavailable-prediction admission rule is specified.

**Counterexample trajectory — constructed:**

1. Bind the deployment and measure the operation coefficients.
2. Introduce a new plan family with zero measured `(W, t)` pairs.
3. Its coefficients yield `W = 0.4` CPU-seconds. The effective elapsed-engine cap is one second.
4. No fitted `g_f` exists for that family.
5. One concretization initializes `t̂ = W` and admits the plan. Another initializes an elapsed-time model accounting for storage waits and predicts four seconds, refusing ordinary admission.
6. The text specifies neither initialization. An “unknown” ratio interval cannot determine which point prediction the admission comparison uses.

With some development observations, a fit can be available before the 100-run interval threshold. The unresolved case is the absence of a usable fit, especially zero samples.

**Consequence:** Most of SC619-01 is repaired, but the method still requires an admission decision from a quantity it has not defined in a material operating regime. Runtime caps constrain subsequent spending; they do not supply the missing pre-execution prediction.

**Bounded repair:** Define the bootstrap prediction and its deployment binding, or explicitly mark elapsed-time prediction unavailable and specify the permitted calibration, refusal or budget-limited behavior until a predictor exists. Keep CPU work, elapsed time and empirical interval status separate. This needs a local method rule, not another planning component.

### SC620-02 — D2: independently reachable parts need not be jointly answerable within one request budget

**Earliest owning domain:** D2. **New evaluation defect; blocking.**

D2 §10.1 fixes the request’s parts, then determines reachability separately:

> For each part, the adjudicator determines, with its **own reference budget** (offline, separate from the product's, and allowed exhaustive scans of the eligible corpus and human judgment), its **reachability within the product budget**:

Its reachable case exhibits an answer to that part:

> together with a retrieval the product's route kinds can execute within the request's effective budget (§7.3), and confirms it by running that retrieval on the product

Section 10.2 defines an unjustified abstention as:

> left unsettled, for any reason, though the part is reachable

The product budget belongs to the request. The reference procedure effectively allows each part to use that whole budget independently. Individual witnesses therefore do not establish a jointly feasible full response.

**Counterexample trajectory — constructed:**

1. A recall request asks for the complete verbatim text of two distinct records, A and B.
2. Bind an effective returned-context allowance of 4,000 tokens. Each requested text requires 2,400 tokens; each can individually be returned, grounded and retrieved within every other cap.
3. The adjudicator runs the retrieval for A and the retrieval for B separately. Each part is therefore reachable under §10.1.
4. A response containing both complete texts would exceed the shared returned-context cap. The system returns A and explicitly identifies the budget-limited remainder.
5. Section 10.2 nevertheless assigns unjustified abstention to B because B was individually reachable.
6. Across 100 requests, suppose ten have this legitimate joint-budget limitation, ten have genuinely avoidable partial answers, and eighty are fully answered. The current rule reports `a_u = 20%`; distinguishing the legitimate budget limitation gives `a_u = 10%`. These fall on opposite sides of the proposed `a_max = 15%`. Even counting all ten legitimate cases as budget-limited stops leaves that share at the proposed `b_max = 10%`.

The same issue arises with shared elapsed-time, internal-token, candidate or returned-item limits. It is not specific to long quotations.

**Consequence:** The evaluator can charge a conforming, budget-honest partial answer for an impossible full response. It can also favor overspending to avoid an abstention penalty. This conflicts with the intended separation between honest budget limitations and avoidable failure in D1 §9 and F-48.

**Bounded repair:** Require joint feasibility under the single request budget when adjudicating whether a full response was reachable. Define how partial-answer omissions are assessed against that shared feasibility, including resources already needed by the delivered answer. A recorded joint retrieval/response witness and a fixed rule for budget-justified partial answers are sufficient; no value-of-information optimizer is required.

Retain the worked example, but state that both parts are **jointly** answerable within the request’s effective budget.

### SC620-03 — D2: an unknown remainder can erase a known confident error from acceptance

**Earliest owning domain:** D2. **New evaluation defect; blocking.**

D2 §10.2 explicitly allows overlapping request indicators:

> Each request then gets five **indicators**, which may overlap:

In particular:

| Indicator | Operative condition |
| --- | --- |
| `wc` | “any part is confident wrong” |
| `unk` | “any part is unknown” |

It then defines the rates:

> The rates `u`, `u_partial`, `e_wc` and `a_u` are the means of `useful`, `useful_partial`, `wc` and `au` over the evaluated requests with `unk = 0`;

Thus `wc = 1` and `unk = 1` can coexist, but that request contributes nothing to `e_wc` or the preference penalty. Unknown reachability of one part removes an already adjudicated confident error about another part.

**Counterexample trajectory — constructed:**

1. Evaluate 100 decision-basis requests, each with two parts.
2. On 96 requests, the response settles both parts correctly and grounded.
3. On four requests, the response confidently states a false answer to part 1. The adjudicator establishes that error. Part 2 has unknown reference reachability and the response leaves it unsettled.
4. Each of those four requests has `wc = 1` and `unk = 1`.
5. All four are excluded from the rate denominator. The reported values are `e_wc = 0%`, `u = 100%` and `a_u = 0%`.
6. The unknown share is 4%, below the proposed `k_max = 5%`. The reported decision-basis error requirement `e_wc ≤ ε_db`, with proposed `ε_db = 1%`, passes even though four of the 100 responses contain established confident errors.

This counterexample needs no false-negative pruning, wrong mutation target or limitation presented as a finding. Those independent defect rules do not close it.

**Consequence:** Reference uncertainty about one question suppresses positive evidence of an error about another. The asymmetric evaluation can assign no confident-error cost to a known confident error. Merely reporting the excluded share does not repair that acceptance rule.

**Bounded repair:** Count established confident errors over a denominator that does not discard them because another part is unknown. Keep reference incompleteness separately visible and bind any conditional usefulness/abstention denominator before observing the responses. Add the mixed `wc = 1`, `unk = 1` case as a fixed example, showing its error contribution and acceptance disposition.

The owner may set tolerable uncertainty and error thresholds. Those choices do not require discarding known errors.

## 3. Other findings by earliest owning domain

### F620-01 — D3: T167 needs to distinguish content invariance from resource-dependent execution coverage

**Earliest owning domain:** D3, coordinating T167 with D2 §7.2 item 3. **Evidence-specification finding; not an independent Gate 0 entry blocker.**

The count-domain correction is substantive. D2 §7.2 item 3 expressly says:

> A count over all segments is not of that kind when segments hold records the reader cannot read.

It also retains a resource residual:

> Hidden content may affect only timing and the admission decision itself, which is the timing and resource side channel of core §10.1, measured separately (annex T167);

T167 now requires hidden-only paired worlds to show:

> the same estimates, the same explanation of them, the same continuation offers and the same coverage

while allowing:

> any difference in timing or in the admission decision

Core §10.1 likewise retains timing and resource side channels. D2 §7.4 requires a runtime stop to report budget-limited execution and its unevaluated part. Those outcomes can change coverage.

**Counterexample trajectory — constructed:**

1. Hold the reader’s eligible corpus, request, rights, safe displayed estimates and budget fixed.
2. Hidden-only work or resource contention changes a permitted resource outcome: one world completes the plan; the other is refused admission or reaches the runtime cap.
3. The completed execution reports completion. The other must report the refusal or incomplete execution.
4. Requiring identical coverage across those outcomes either fails T167 or conceals the actual limitation.

**Consequence:** T167 overstates the equality needed across executions that its own residual permits to differ. This is a conflict in the evidence specification, not a demonstrated implementation disclosure.

**Bounded repair:** Define which observations must remain invariant under equivalent execution conditions, and separately exercise a resource-outcome boundary. Require actual execution coverage to remain honest, keep hidden records out of all exposed counts and explanations, and identify any permitted resource-status difference as the retained residual. Reconcile the continuation wording at the same boundary. Do not silently broaden the residual to expose hidden content.
