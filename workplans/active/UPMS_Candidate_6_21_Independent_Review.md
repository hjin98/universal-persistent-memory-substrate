## 4. Falsification of the joint-count simplification

D2 §10.1 defines `k*` as:

> the largest number of parts that **one** response can settle correctly and grounded within the request's effective budget (§7.3), all components shared.

Section 10.2 defines `au` by:

> `k*` is known and `k_c < k*`

These rules survive the following distinctions.

### 4.1 Different parts from the witness

Suppose three parts each require 1,900 returned tokens and the shared limit is 4,000. A witness settles parts 1 and 2, establishing a feasible count of two; the maximum is two. A product response settling parts 2 and 3 also has `k_c = k* = 2`.

It is correctly uncharged. The witness’s particular subset need not be prescribed to the product. If an additional part could be settled alongside the delivered two within the budget, two would not be the true maximum.

The simplification therefore does not require per-part witness identity merely to compare achieved cardinality with the maximum.

### 4.2 Maximum is stronger than inability to append another part

A witness alone establishes a lower bound. Showing that no further part fits alongside that witness establishes local maximality, which need not establish the required largest count.

For example, with costs of 3,500, 1,900 and 1,900 tokens under a 4,000-token limit:

- the first part alone cannot be extended;
- the second and third parts jointly fit;
- the required maximum is two.

The operative word **“largest”** rules out reporting one as `k*`. The reference procedure must establish that maximum, rather than merely produce a nonextendible response. Where maximum reachability cannot be established, the unknown-reference path remains necessary. The executable adjudication rubric should make this distinction explicit.

This is an evidence obligation under the existing definition, not justification for adding a product optimizer.

### 4.3 The simplification makes a substantive preference explicit

In the unequal-cost example, a response settling only the expensive first part receives `au = 1`, even if no further part can be added to that particular response. Another response could settle two parts.

Thus the method measures a shortfall against **maximum cardinality**, rather than only avoidable omissions conditional on preserving the delivered subset. That is a coherent proposed policy within the D1 hand-off, but the owner should understand it before ratification. Atomic-part definitions must be frozen before evaluation; changing their granularity could change the maximum and the resulting charge.

The rule also retains a deliberate reference-coverage limit: `a_u` is conditional on known `k*`, while `e_wc` includes every evaluated request. The unknown share must remain visible and bounded as specified; conditional `a_u` must not be presented as an unconditional population rate.

No new arithmetic loophole was established by these cases.
