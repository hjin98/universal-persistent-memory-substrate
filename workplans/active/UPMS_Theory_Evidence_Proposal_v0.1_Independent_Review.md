### SC-TE01 — Exact judgment revisions do not constitute a current-standing dependency contract

**Owner:** D1 standing/warrant semantics, then D3 derivation and admission. **Text:** P §§3.6, 5.6–5.7, 6.1, 13.3; C5 §§5.5, 9.3; SSDS §§4.1–4.3, 5.5–5.9.

P §6.1 proposes both a read-time rule, “C is supported if premises P1 and P2 are supported and the step is valid,” and stored judgments citing exact judgments, becoming stale when those judgments are superseded. This is a useful direction but is not the contract C5 deferred.

**Trajectory A: an unchanged revision loses currentness.** Jp supports P. Jc relies on Jp. New evidence or a ruleset change makes Jp ineffective under C5 §9.3 without superseding Jp's immutable revision. Jc still names the same revision. A test of revision identity/supersession alone cannot establish Jp's effective currentness or Jc's. A transitive check of current scope epochs, rules and obligations could reject Jc, but P does not require that dependency meaning; it claims to have supplied it. A new opposing judgment also need not supersede the cited favorable judgment, and C5 explicitly excludes judgment publication from evidence-epoch advancement. A projection that selects the cited favorable judgment can miss newly recorded disagreement unless its dependencies include the relevant judgment set, not only the cited IDs.

**Trajectory B: cycles and initialization.** C5 permits cyclic evidence relations. For a support component with P supported iff Q is supported and Q supported iff P is supported, both all-unsupported and all-supported satisfy those Boolean equations. “Compute at read time” chooses neither. A cached supported state seeded by earlier evidence can persist after that evidence is invalidated unless cycle handling and recomputation semantics forbid it. TE-07 correctly rejects circular warrant, but a cycle *flag* in §6.2 is not an evaluation rule. Immutable production ancestry being a DAG does not make the semantic support graph acyclic. Conversely, exact references to strictly earlier judgment records can be acyclic; a cycle is not inevitable merely because judgments cite judgments.

**Trajectory C: incompatibility with the retained base.** C5 §9.3 refuses admission for an assessment whose ancestry includes a judgment, projection or a descendant of either. It does not merely forbid a field called “current status.” P changes the intended policy but leaves unclear which ancestral prohibitions are replaced, whether a cited judgment is historical evidence or a live prerequisite, and how projected status may enter a new assessment. Moving a calculation from a stored field into a view does not remove its epistemic effect.

**Required disposition:** define dependency modes, currentness and cycle semantics before accepting §6.1. The bounded proposal in §4 below suffices; importing the SSDS orchestration engine does not. Recheck unchanged-but-stale prerequisites, additional adverse judgments, mixed scopes, partial traversal and cached versus from-scratch evaluation. Confidence: high that the contract is incomplete; no claim of realized flicker or an unavoidable cyclic implementation.

### SC-TE02 — The sealed-chair promise conflicts with inherited oversight and has no adequate trust boundary

**Owner:** D3 security and principal architecture; product owner for the changed oversight promise. **Text:** P §§7.1, 8.1–8.2, 13.3, TE-15; C5 §§7.3.1, 7.3.4, 7.3.8, 10.6.5; C5 AC3/AC7 and D-9.

P says an agent chair reads true identities, no human sees them when the chair is an agent, and administrators cannot read sealed attribution. It retains agents with human overseers, retains team-memory provisions, and does not list D-9 as superseded. C5 caps an agent principal's read reach at its human overseer's non-elevated reach. The two requirements cannot both hold for the seal: either the overseer can read it or the agent cannot. A separately confined service role could fix this, but that is a D3 change to principal and trust ownership, not a serialization choice for D4.

An administrator excluded from a normal read API may still control role assignment, chair replacement, storage, backup restore, invocation tracing, model credentials or the host. P supplies no boundary distinguishing an ordinary application administrator from those operators. True identity may also occur in `witness_principal`, ownership, source locators, submission logs, manifests and the chair's model request. Sealing only an attribution column does not seal those paths. Removing the full runtime from the core does not make the chair's identity processing external to the promise.

**Required disposition:** specify the protected mapping and all replicas/metadata paths, the trusted chair service and processor, who can grant its capability, replacement/recovery, and precisely which administrators/operators are inside the threat model. Explicitly reconcile D-9. If protection includes hostile host or key administrators, establish an architecture meeting that threat model before admitting sealed identities; otherwise obtain an owner-approved narrower guarantee. Do not present general anonymity against inference from writing style as guaranteed: P already calls that best effort. No particular encryption or enclave implementation is prescribed here.

The oversight contradiction is demonstrated. Actual operator compromise, log exposure and provider retention failures are plausible risks, not observed incidents. Confidence: high.

### SC-TE03 — Screening is promoted from a fallible cue into a guarantee against dissent loss and person characterization

**Owner:** D3 publication/view boundary, with D1 fidelity requirements. **Text:** P §§6.2, 6.4, 11, TE-02/TE-13; C5 §§9.7, 10.5.3.

C5 explicitly says its screening is high-precision, low-recall, does not screen all text, and cannot certify narrative faithfulness. P preserves that disclaimer in §6.2, but §11 says omission of any live alternative or unresolved objection is flagged by the same contradiction check, and person-characterizing narratives are flagged and not published.

**Omission counterexample:** exact records contain H and an unresolved objection O. A narrative says only “H explains the observations.” O's omission need not introduce an incompatible typed value or any logical contradiction. The narrative can pass slot screening while losing dissent.

**Characterization counterexample:** “The calculations bearing author X's signature are best left to more careful hands.” This is an adverse person characterization without a reliability field or explicit numerical score. A semantic detector might catch it, but no declared sound procedure establishes that it always will. Conversely, an exact, narrow report of an error in one work may be misclassified as a personal attack and suppress legitimate critique. A short bounded hub cannot literally include every item in an unbounded objection set either.

**Required disposition:** keep the ban on system judgments of people (D-32), but separate enforceable output construction from semantic detection. Mandatory decision-facing status, dissent and objection identities can come from deterministic projection, with pagination and explicit coverage; free prose remains qualified synthesis. Use a constrained or reviewed publication path where the product promises more. State detector coverage, residual risk, fallback and appeal behavior. “No flag” must not certify TE-13 or complete dissent preservation. This challenges the proposed guarantee mechanism, not the owner's choice to prohibit person scoring. Confidence: high.

### SC-TE04 — “Anonymous objections never block” needs an explicit boundary around independently established invalidity

**Owner:** D1 consequential-use and objection semantics; owner decision D-36 where interpreted absolutely. **Text:** P §§5.6–5.7, 7.1, TE-07/TE-15; S §4.4 and §8.3.

An anonymous critic supplies a concrete counterexample to a theorem used in a proposed finalization. The counterexample can be checked from already public definitions, without knowing its author's identity. Checking confirms that the relied-on inference is invalid. The critic remains anonymous.

It is coherent to give the anonymous record **no procedural veto**. It is not coherent to use its anonymity to preserve an unqualified warrant that has now failed. If “never blocks a decision, finalization or publication” means that even verified consequences may not stop the dependent unqualified use, D-36 conflicts with the retained warrant and review-obligation rules. If it means only that the objection record is not a blocking policy input, a fresh attributable assessment can remove support and the conflict disappears. P does not settle this distinction. A PI may still publish a contested or qualified outcome; that is different from labeling the invalid inference supported.

The rationale that anonymous critics probably have little stake also fails as a general premise: a junior coauthor may know the decisive defect and face the greatest career cost of signing. That is a thought experiment about the policy's scope, not a measured sociological claim.

**Required owner disposition:** affirm the narrow no-veto interpretation while retaining ordinary evidence/applicability consequences regardless of who supplied the lead. Decide whether a consequential decision must explicitly record examination of a material anonymous objection, without giving its author a veto. If D-36 prohibits even independently verified invalidity from affecting eligibility, this review raises a **SERIOUS CHALLENGE to that reading of D-36 itself**. Do not silently require the critic to sign to restore scientific integrity. Confidence: high on the ambiguity and counterexample; behavior in practice unmeasured.

### SC-TE05 — Recorded decisions can become “current” without a defined authority and concurrency contract

**Owner:** D3 SCIRE–UPMS interface, constrained by D1/project authority rules. **Text:** P §§3.5, 6.1, 7, 12; S §§2.2–2.3, 4.4–4.5; R V4-14/V4-20.

P correctly leaves scientific permission to finalize with SCIRE and distinguishes it from account access. Yet §3.5 retains coordination decisions as “recorded but never shown as in force,” while §6.1 permits a position to show as current where a recorded decision makes it so. There is no defined distinction between a reported external decision, a proposed decision, and a SCIRE decision whose required authority and signatures have been validated.

**Trajectory:** two PIs must jointly sign a decision. PI A signs versions C1 and basis B1. Before PI B signs, a basis changes or an objection is added; B signs a later view. All signatures, objections and exposure records can be retained, satisfying the listed storage invariants, without anyone having jointly approved one exact decision. A front end that merely stores the fields has no specified atomic precondition or invalidation rule. Similarly, an ordinary memory client can record an assertion that a PI finalized C; UPMS must not turn that into an effective scientific choice.

Keeping policy in SCIRE is correct. The missing element is a **versioned policy result and conditional transition contract**: exact decision content, policy revision, authority evidence, required signer set, signatures over that same content, dependency/objection coverage snapshot, and revalidation at commit. The core need not judge scientific materiality; it must preserve and atomically enforce the structural preconditions supplied by the authorized policy owner. Other front ends may store reported decisions but cannot manufacture SCIRE effectiveness.

**Required disposition:** define historical-recorded versus validated-for-use status, overlap/disagreement behavior, and the backend primitives supporting SCIRE's rule. Keep “current” explicitly scoped to a project decision, not epistemic truth. Confidence: high that these material boundaries are missing; the race is a constructed execution, not a tested implementation.

## 3. Model adequacy: preserve the axes, complete their typing

**The sound core.** Event/Claim/Judgment separates occurrence, propositional content and assessment. A theory can originate in a recorded conjecture rather than an observation of nature; an experimental observation can depend on an instrument model. Keeping evidential and inferential support separate, under explicit conditions, handles this without inventing an evidence/theory dichotomy. Questions can organize several positions without owning every record. Modality, interpretation/confirmation/adoption, comparative versus absolute evidence, use history, contested meaning, and discovery versus justification are strong choices. One store reduces duplicated custody and transport work. D-20 and D-22 stand.

“Three record types” should describe semantic axes, not literally the exhaustive object count: Question, Objection, Decision, typed relations and controls also have identities and lifecycles. No need to turn every coordination object into another belief.

**Scientific work requiring a clearer mapping:**

- A statistical analysis uses dataset D, calibration K, definition U and subset-selection history H. A later correction to K or U may invalidate an evidence relation although D is unchanged. P §5.6 promises traversal over provenance/evidential/inferential graphs, but does not explicitly include asserted meaning, model and condition dependencies. S §6.6 did include meaning links. Merely storing a definition inside an Event does not create the dependent edges. Add their exact identities to the actual relation being assessed; distinguish semantic reliance from exposure.
- A reviewer assesses whether an inference or evidence relation is valid, or whether a resolution answered an objection. Those are not necessarily assessments of a single target claim. Either generalize the judgment target to a typed versioned object, or reify a proposition about that object and make it the sole assessment owner. Do not keep an independently editable relation status alongside a conflicting Judgment.
- A mathematical definition can be recursive; a theorem can be proved by induction. Recursion in a definition is not circular evidential support. A proof's terminal assumptions and imported theorem hypotheses must be available and propagated. An Event recording an axiom establishes that it was stated, not its truth or an unconditional proof root. P §2.1 understands this; §§5.7/6.2 need the same distinction operationally.
- A model valid in one regime and a measurement valid in another cannot be combined simply because both have a supported label. Nor can ordinal support be multiplied, voted on, or treated as a calibrated probability. Keep type-specific warrant, scope, conditions, uncertainty and dependence attached to every propagated conclusion. Missing premises or unknown selection history remain explicit gaps.
- A joint project decision, a quarantined personal critique and a sealed reviewer identity need operational records, not merely more scientific claims. The axes can represent their evidence, but effectiveness and visibility require the distinct control contracts identified above.

These are representational and semantic closure needs, not evidence that one memory cannot serve science. I found no decisive need to split theory and evidence back into separate stores. The model must, however, support scientific artifacts and exact locators beyond prose: datasets, figures, code and proof outputs must remain canonical payloads/references; “original text is the record” cannot justify replacing them with an extracted description. C5's payload and media reconstruction provisions provide a starting point.

## 4. Proposed bounded answer to open item 6: standing and judgment-on-judgment

**Accept the architectural direction conditionally; reject the claim that §6.1 already closes it.** A minimal contract can use the existing records and commit owner:

1. **Distinguish dependence meanings.** Recording that J was shown, citing J as a historical fact, relying on J's assessed content, and requiring J to remain currently usable are different dependencies. P already separates exposure from support; extend that distinction to judgment references. A historical decision keeps its historical basis even when current support fails.
2. **Fix the evaluation boundary.** A standing query binds a canonical commit position, policy/ruleset/projection version, scope, target revisions, audience and coverage. Within that boundary it is deterministic. A later observation legitimately changes a later answer; incoherent mixed-snapshot answers are not acceptable “flicker.” Current-state presentation must check currentness rather than silently reuse an old as-of result.
3. **Define invalidators, including negative information.** Current reliance depends on prerequisite currentness, applicable evidence epochs, active rules, review obligations and relevant adverse/disputed judgments. A change to the set of applicable objections or judgments can invalidate a view even when none of its named revisions changed. Track this through the existing core's epochs/dependency checks, not an unrelated second state registry.
4. **Give support a well-founded interpretation.** The simplest first product accepts acyclic, explicit support routes for current warrants. It may retain cyclic proposals as data with no positive warrant through the cycle. Definition recursion is separate. If a later product needs fixed-point semantics, define its domain, monotonicity, cycle treatment and unique selected solution before claiming it. Do not adopt arbitrary fixed points or cache initialization as semantics.
5. **Keep disagreement and qualifiers.** Return compatible assessments by scope/ruleset with opposing judgments and validity conditions. Do not select a favorable witness while suppressing an applicable adverse assessment; do not automatically convert “contested” into “false.” A failed route need not destroy a sufficient independent route, but the failed route and its review obligation remain visible.
6. **Make truncation safe.** When the bounded traversal cannot establish a route, return partial/under-review and the coverage reason; never use an omitted dependency as satisfied. Paginate records instead of pretending a bounded narrative contains all dissent. A known independent complete route may support a carefully scoped claim if the policy explicitly permits it and adverse coverage is adequate.
7. **Reconcile admission explicitly.** Replace the appropriate part of C5 §9.3 and E3 rather than retaining contradictory prose. Stored Jc may rely on an exact Jp only with its declared dependency mode and live validation where needed. Projections remain derived; reading one neither supplies independent evidence nor strips its ancestry. Projection-derived paraphrases cannot launder an invalid basis.
8. **Reproduce history.** “As held then” uses then-recorded state and then-rules; “as now assessed about then” uses current assessments over historical objects. Preserve the needed version identities and report deletion/capture gaps. A read-time projection alone does not store what a decider actually received; preserve the exposure separately.

SSDS supplies a useful contrast, not a ready-made proof: A §4.1 has `identity`, `record`, `standing`, `content`, `validity` and `execution` basis modes; §5.6 explicitly handles support cycles; §5.8 isolates non-monotone consequences. P borrows only exact references while omitting those discriminating meanings. UPMS can adopt a smaller contract suitable for inquiry, with independent review of that contract.

## 5. Security, people, retention and practical limits

### 5.1 What D-24 can simplify, and what remains needed

Moving external action execution and C0–C4 dispatch policy to the runtime is sensible. It does not remove UPMS's responsibility for its own retrieval, model processing, write, export, backup and role boundaries. Data-never-instructions is an invariant to enforce through capabilities and output paths, not a filter proving arbitrary prose harmless.

The default profile may deliberately trust authorized writers to share responsibly. Its honest guarantee is **direct access control over stored records**, with attributable sharing and declared derivative-containment limits. It cannot simultaneously promise that unpublished work stays within a project merely because that source's scope is private. This is a consequence of the chosen simplification, not a request to restore all candidate 5 machinery.

D-27 can stand if the first product explicitly excludes embargoed, human-subject, industry-restricted and other material requiring derivative containment. Restricted projects need the strict profile **before ingesting or exposing restricted content**, not after a leak demonstrates demand. Even open projects contain private submissions, authorship disputes and sealed identities; these retained features need their own bounded protection now or must wait with an explicit unavailable state. This is not legal advice or a claim about any particular project's legal duties.

The later strict rule—“content written in a session that read the project stays in that project unless its owner shares it”—is not yet sufficient. For a session reading A and B with different source owners, no single destination or unspecified “owner” resolves both constraints. It needs source-specific authorization, purpose/processor limits, context continuations/forks, unknown lineage, revocation and output coverage. A source may permit reading but not retention even inside a new project. These are surviving SC-43/V4-08 concerns. Any future strict experiment must reject both leakage and unacceptable over-blocking; D-24 does not resolve that tradeoff by definition.

Keeping restriction epochs only for correction/deletion also leaves direct permission revocation underspecified. Define the last permitted release and acknowledgment semantics for a removed project member, including queued responses. This can be a small direct-read fence; it need not restore full runtime taint.

**Truth-based robustness:** P correctly says injection, disclosure and flooding need separate defenses. The first rows remain too strong. A falsehood in unstructured text need not raise a flag; an authentic account can supply false evidence; common ancestry can be unrecorded; accurate cherry-picked observations can bias a conclusion. Say that traceable records and assessed evidence *help investigate some attacks*. No experiment supports “most attacks” or a generally strong integrity defense. Source classes identify how bytes arrived, not whether the author is honest or the content true.

### 5.2 People, anonymity and chair governance

D-32 is implementable as a product policy over explicit data products: no person score, author leaderboard, institution ranking or author-derived work quality. It is not a theorem that arbitrary agent prose or allowed channel metadata cannot characterize people indirectly. Define prohibited proxy constructions such as an “instrument channel” whose only purpose is aggregating one lab's work, and a “dataset” consisting of one author's assessed papers. Keep channel assessment tied to a technical measurement/error mechanism and a claim class. Track record also needs the denominator and coverage of later assessments, including failures and unassessed work; preferential checking of disfavored sources can bias an otherwise independent assessment collection.

P's statement that authorship is “never an input” to assessment must distinguish **quality scoring** from the explicitly required authorship checks for independence and conflicts. Reading authorship to disqualify self-review is necessary and should not be treated as forbidden person scoring. Person-directed human claims should have a distinct signed-record rendering path with replies and no proactive ranking, not be turned into the agent's own view. A reply being stored somewhere is insufficient: when a displayed claim has a reply, show its existence and permitted content under the same audience rules, or qualify the display.

Sealed attribution needs an identity-assurance statement: authenticated account is not necessarily a unique human; aliases, imported critiques and unverified identity cannot establish independence. A chair can attest bounded independence without releasing identities, but public exact deduplication or conflict results may reveal them by differencing. For example, an audience knows the author of one critique and learns that this plus one anonymous critique has exactly one author. It has learned the anonymous author's identity. Limit disclosure and query scope accordingly; do not merely hide the underlying table.

A fresh chair context is useful for separating prior discussion exposure; it does not establish neutrality of its appointing operator, prompts, processor, family or tools. The default appointment can remain automatic under D-35, but its authority must be a pre-existing role, not granted by an anonymous message's content. Human assignments need the same conflict check as defaults. Later conflicts, chair failure, replacement and seal continuity need declared outcomes. If no eligible human can hear an appeal, record it pending; do not manufacture neutrality or silently turn a review chair into scientific adjudicator. An appeal's text may itself identify the writer, so “the appeal reviews text, not identity” is not sufficient sanitization.

Identity masking and independent falsification are also different. A double-blind reviewer must see the artifact to assess it; excluding all ancestry from its author would remove the artifact itself. C5 §10.6.4 deliberately excludes specified reasoning lineage while admitting that the artifact may contain rationale. P needs both contracts, with separate allowed inputs and coverage, rather than claiming masking “reuses” lineage exclusion without adaptation.

### 5.3 Archive and deletion

Never-delete-unasked is feasible **with finite admission and explicit capacity decisions**. If new retained data arrive at rate b and storage capacity is B, moving data to a cold tier does not remove the eventual capacity constraint. The product needs quota accounting, bounded indexes/projections, refusal/backpressure before acknowledging data it cannot retain, reserved space for controls, and owner choices to expand, export or explicitly delete. There is no need to restore time-based deletion of scientific records.

Clarify two meanings of archive: C5's canonical corpus below the hubs and P's newly inactive/cold records. Every active record already belongs to the former. The latter should be a reversible visibility/storage-tier classification, not a second corpus with different truth rules. Relevance, live dependents and explicit pins may rank recommendations, but popularity must not make an adverse record disappear from mandatory decision or standing views. “Dependents reviewed” needs an outcome, not merely an acknowledgment. Operational scratch files and reproducible indexes also need a clear lifecycle distinct from canonical scientific records; D-25 need not require retaining every temporary representation forever.
